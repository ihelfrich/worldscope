## Headline of the week

A Thanksgiving week that opened with a historic equity shock and closed with diplomatic contradiction: on Monday November 23, U.S. markets staged one of their largest single-session recoveries of the year, with the Nasdaq 100 up 7.0 percent and the S&P 500 up 4.7 percent, apparently on renewed Ukraine-Russia ceasefire signaling and expectations of a U.S.-China trade framework. By Wednesday, the same week produced a fresh round of U.S. tariffs hitting China at its highest rate yet, an Embraer aircraft levy, a first fatality at Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery from a continuing Houthi campaign, a reported Iranian drone killing two Italians in Kuwait, and Trump publicly rewriting the NATO-Ukraine joint communique to call for negotiations. The week ended with the 10-year Treasury at 4.91 percent, the 30-year at 5.30 percent, vol elevated (VXX up 3.97 percent at mid-week), and small-cap domestic equities systematically outperforming large-cap tech across every session after Monday. The regime that looked briefly like a risk-on breakout became, by Friday, a more complicated story about geopolitical risk premiums, fiscal durability, and what happens to asset prices when peace signals and tariff signals arrive in the same week.

## Macro: how the picture moved

The rate configuration as of Friday is unambiguous in one direction: the yield curve has re-steepened marginally (10y-2y spread 0.24, up from near-zero in prior months), but the absolute level of long rates is the story. The 30-year Treasury at 5.30 percent, the 10-year at 4.91 percent, and the Fed funds effective rate at 3.88 percent produce a term premium that has not been this wide in this cycle. The 203 basis point spread between the front end and the long end signals that markets are demanding compensation for fiscal risk, not just for expected rate paths. The Fed balance sheet stood at $6.74 trillion as of November 18, still far above pre-COVID levels, and M2 at $23.5 trillion in October reflects the structural liquidity backdrop.

On the inflation front, the latest available data (October, released during this week) shows the CPI headline index at 337.5 and core PCE at 131.3. These are level figures, not rates-of-change, and the daily feed does not carry month-over-month calculations. What the structure does reveal is that the Fed at 3.88 percent effective is well below the long end, and prediction markets give negligible probability to a December rate change. The December meeting on December 9 is essentially priced as a hold.

The dollar finished the week mixed. EUR/USD moved from 1.0981 (Thursday FRED data) to a range near 1.013-1.015 (proxied by the FXE ETF at 101.38, +0.12 percent Thursday). The yen at 158.76 per dollar remains a persistent signal of Bank of Japan divergence. The yuan at 6.677 is notably weaker than its prior-year range; combined with the new tariff round, the PBOC's willingness to hold that level will bear watching into December. WTI crude at $82.24 on Tuesday (FRED) is consistent with the Wednesday oil ETF (USO) surge of 3.29 percent, the week's most significant commodity move, almost certainly driven by the Jazan refinery attack.

The key macro inference of the week: the United States is operating with a fiscal trajectory that the bond market is refusing to discount away. The 30-year at 5.30 percent while the front end is at 3.88 percent is not a soft-landing yield curve. It is a curve that says long-duration fiscal risk is being priced in real time.

## Markets: rotation and dispersion

The week's market story is best told in three phases, with the most consequential being what happened before Tuesday.

**The selloff that entered the week.** Both the Sunday (November 22) and Monday (November 23) morning bundles showed Friday November 21's close in full: SPY 689.81 (down 3.64 percent on the week), QQQ 608.45 (down 5.67 percent), EEM 53.84 (down 5.13 percent). Markets came into Thanksgiving week having already absorbed a sharp drawdown. The catalyst is not confirmed in the available feeds, but the magnitude and breadth of the decline -- international equities (EFA -2.67%), EM (-5.13%), and U.S. large-cap all down together -- suggests macro shock rather than sector rotation.

**The Monday recovery.** Tuesday's bundle (reflecting Monday November 23 close) showed the reversal: SPY +4.71 percent to 722.29, QQQ +7.00 percent to 651.03, IWM +3.56 percent to 265.74, EEM +7.41 percent to 57.83. The EM surge of 7.41 percent in a single session is statistically unusual and hard to explain through purely U.S. domestic channels. The simultaneous dollar strengthening (DXY proxy UUP +0.54 percent) alongside EM gains is atypical; risk-on moves typically come with dollar weakness. The most coherent explanation, consistent with the Ukraine theater and Russian internal feeds, is that weekend diplomatic signaling on a potential U.S.-Russia-Ukraine framework (following the November 10-11 Abu Dhabi trilateral) drove a broad relief rally. The dollar could have strengthened separately on the tariff-bargaining-chip logic.

**Wednesday-Thursday: rotation and risk repricing.** The Wednesday bundle showed QQQ -1.99 percent while IWM gained 0.53 percent -- the classic rotation trade from mega-cap tech toward domestic small cap. The long-bond ETF (TLT) gained 0.92 percent on Wednesday, the front-end (SHY) gained 0.09 percent, and VXX surged 3.97 percent. That combination -- bonds up, vol up, tech down, small cap up -- is consistent with a geopolitical shock (Jazan attack first fatality, Kuwait drone incident) arriving simultaneously with the new tariff announcement, forcing a risk-regime re-evaluation. Oil spiking 3.29 percent while bonds rallied confirms the geopolitical read rather than a pure deflation signal.

By Thursday (Thanksgiving; markets closed), the cumulative picture was: SPY gained approximately 3.9 percent from Friday November 21 to Wednesday November 25, with QQQ gaining a net 4.5 percent. But tech relative to small cap compressed substantially: IWM was up approximately 4.9 percent over the same window, outperforming QQQ. This small-cap outperformance has been persistent across every full trading session of the week after Monday.

Gold (GLD) finished at $327.45 (+0.61 percent Wednesday), consistent with elevated geopolitical risk premium. High yield (HYG) was essentially flat to slightly positive (+0.12 percent), suggesting credit markets are not signaling stress even as equities vol elevated. The divergence between equity vol and credit spread is worth monitoring heading into December.

## Geopolitical signal

The dominant geopolitical signal of the week is a conjunction of three distinct escalatory events that all arrived in the same 72-hour window (Wednesday-Friday): the Jazan refinery attack fatality, the Kuwait drone incident, and the new U.S.-China tariff round.

**Jazan refinery.** The Saudi Energy Ministry confirmed one dead and several injured in a November 27 attack on the Jazan refinery, the facility that has been targeted repeatedly in the Houthi campaign since July 2026. This is the first confirmed fatality at the installation. Prior attacks in July (refinery shutdown), August, and September 8 (73 wounded; refinery offline) established the pattern; the fatality escalates the threshold. At $82/barrel WTI, the market is already pricing in some Middle East risk premium; a confirmed operational disruption at a 400,000 bpd facility would reprice crude substantially.

**Kuwait drone.** Iran denied involvement in a drone attack that killed two Italian nationals in Kuwait, as reported in the conflict feed on November 27. The specific location has not been confirmed through open sources available to this desk. The sequence through 2026 (Ali Al Salem Air Base attack March 15, Iran-Kuwait drone exchange April, Kuwait City airport attack June) suggests a continuing Iran-proxy campaign against Gulf infrastructure and foreign personnel.

**Ukraine.** The Ukraine theater item count spiked to 393 on Friday, 44 percent above the 7-day median of 273, and Russian internal media is running 1,168 items versus a 943 median. ISW's operational assessments from this week identify 124 daily combat clashes, with Pokrovsk (16), Kostiantynivka (12), and Lyman (5) as the highest-friction axes. Ukrainian drone strikes reached St. Petersburg, Krasnodar, Leningrad, and Tula oblasts overnight Thursday-Friday. Russia responded by classifying footage of drone strikes as a state secret (up to five years imprisonment for filming or leaking such imagery). **China-Taiwan.** Chinese internal media ran 322 items on Friday (versus 271 median), driven by: Shenzhou-24 three-man crew launch (23 echo outlets) and state media coverage of the new tariff round. **Timothy Taylor (Conversable Economist), "The Global Office and the Third Great Unbundling" (November 24).** Builds on Richard Baldwin's unbundling framework: goods trade was the first unbundling, offshoring of production tasks the second, and AI-enabled remote cognitive work the third. **Timothy Taylor (Conversable Economist), "Interview with Lars Peter Hansen: Decision-making Under Uncertainty" (November 25).** Hansen's work on ambiguity aversion and robustness in macroeconomic models is unusually relevant this week. The week's market dynamics -- VXX spike concurrent with bond rally and equity vol -- fit Hansen's framework better than standard risk models: agents were not just repricing risk, they were responding to ambiguity (uncertainty about the probability distribution itself, not just expected values). ## Cross-section themes

**1. The tariff-peace paradox.** This week established with unusual clarity that the same administration is pursuing, simultaneously, a de-escalation channel toward Russia (and by extension Ukraine peace) and an escalation channel toward China and third-country trading partners (Brazil aircraft, fresh China tariff round). These two channels have contradictory market implications. The Monday equity surge (risk-on relief on peace signals) and the Wednesday risk-off reprice (tariff shock) happening within 48 hours of each other is not coincidence -- it is the market trying and failing to discount two incompatible policy regimes. The regime that dominates December will determine whether the 10-year stays at 4.91 or breaks above 5.

**2. Fiscal term premium as the persistent signal.** The 30-year at 5.30, the 10-year at 4.91, the fed funds at 3.88. The 203 basis point spread between front and long end has appeared in this brief multiple weeks now and is widening, not narrowing. The November 25 EO on federal contracting and the CBP supply chain disclosure rule both expand regulatory footprint without revenue offset. The FEC table shows the opposition is raising extraordinary sums -- Talarico at $146 million in Texas, Ossoff at $136 million in Georgia -- which argues for continued policy volatility through 2027 regardless of near-term outcomes. The bond market is pricing that volatility.

**3. The Ukraine war as background radiation.** The 124 daily combat clashes, the drone attacks on St. Petersburg, the ISW assessments (Pokrovsk, Kostiantynivka, Lyman) continue at a pace that the weekly cadence normalizes. What is not normal: the Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian territory reaching new geographic depth (St. Petersburg is on the edge of the operational envelope), and the Russian legislative response (criminalizing footage of drone strikes) suggests the information environment inside Russia is being tightened as internal acknowledgment of vulnerability increases. Lukashenko's mid-week admission that he was shown the attack map before the February 2024 invasion -- captured in Russian internal media -- is a significant historical record addition, whatever its legal implications.

**4. EM-USD decoupling signal.** EEM up 7.41 percent on Monday while the dollar strengthened (+0.54%) is a rare configuration. If confirmed as structurally driven (not a data artifact), it suggests EM is being repriced on factors other than the dollar carry -- possibly China domestic stimulus, commodity demand, or geopolitical de-escalation premiums in specific markets. This would represent a regime shift in the EM/USD correlation that has held for most of the past decade. One week of data is not enough to confirm, but the magnitude warrants watching.

**5. The Jazan-Kuwait-Iran nexus.** Three distinct but potentially connected events: Houthi attack (Jazan first fatality), alleged Iranian drone killing two Italians in Kuwait, and Iran denying both. The Houthi campaign and the Iran-proxy network are operationally distinct but share a patron. The Jazan fatality crosses a threshold that prior attacks had not. If Saudi Arabia escalates its response to the Houthi campaign, the oil risk premium embedded in WTI at $82 becomes a floor, not a ceiling.
