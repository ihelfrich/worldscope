## Headline of the Week

The defining arc of 2026-W26 was the partial settlement of the Iran risk premium that has dominated global markets since late February. A 60-day ceasefire framework signed June 15 matured into concrete sanctions relief on June 22 — a 60-day OFAC waiver covering Iranian oil exports — and by June 24 WTI crude had fallen below $70 per barrel for the first time since the war began, a 37-percent collapse from its April 3 peak of $111.54. That single price signal rippled across every other asset class this week: precious metals cratered, equity volatility climbed, long-duration Treasuries rallied, and the Russian equity market suffered its worst single-session loss since September 2022. Meanwhile, the US Congress passed a war powers resolution for the first time in American history, the United Kingdom lost its sixth prime minister in seven years, Colombia turned hard right at the ballot box, and Venezuela was struck by twin magnitude-7.5 and 7.2 earthquakes that killed more than 235 people. It was a week in which several simultaneous regime shifts — in energy, in UK politics, in Latin American political alignment, in Russian financial markets — reinforced each other rather than competing for attention.

## Macro: How the Picture Moved

The dominant macro event of the week was not a data release but a price level: WTI crossing back below $70. At the start of the week the FRED series showed crude at $84.65 (June 15 reading); by June 22 it had dropped to $78.94; and intraday by June 24 spot prices reached the $67–$70 range. The USO ETF fell 5.7 percent over the four trading days observed. This is not a cyclical demand signal — it is the mechanical unwinding of a war premium that briefly pushed Brent to $118 in late April. The 67 million barrels sitting on tankers in the Gulf, freed by the OFAC waiver, represent supply that markets had been treating as inaccessible; their re-entry repriced the whole forward curve.

The rate picture is subtly more complex than the commodity story. The fed funds effective rate held at 3.63 throughout the week with SOFR at 3.62, confirming no intra-meeting action. But the shape of the curve shifted modestly: the 2-year Treasury rallied from 4.19 to 4.16, while the 10-year rose from 4.46 to 4.50 and the 30-year from 4.90 to 4.94. The 10y2y spread widened from 27 to 30 basis points — bear steepening at the margin. Long bonds simultaneously rallied in ETF terms (TLT up 1.5 percent on the week), which reflects flight-to-duration buying as equities sold off, not a growth expectation change. The net read: the market is repricing away from the inflationary oil-shock scenario that had been embedding itself since the spring, while holding the view that the Fed will stay on hold through the summer. Labor data remain stale (4.3 percent unemployment, 159,001 thousand payrolls, both as of May) and will be the next significant update to watch.

Gold fell 4.9 percent on the week (GLD: $384.59 to $365.92); silver fell 12.1 percent (SLV: $58.91 to $51.78). Both moves are consistent with the partial resolution of the geopolitical uncertainty that had driven safe-haven flows since the war began. VIX rose from 16.78 on June 19 to 19.49 on June 23 — notable but not yet in stress territory. The simultaneous fall in gold and rise in VIX is the tell: this is not a simple risk-on move. Markets are repricing one specific risk (Middle East war) while becoming more uncertain about the macro regime that underlies it.

## Markets: Rotation and Dispersion

Equities sold off across the week with significant dispersion by sector and cap size. The S&P 500 (SPY) fell 1.5 percent from 744.39 to 733.24. The Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) underperformed sharply, down 3.7 percent from 737.95 to 710.62 — the largest weekly decline in the tech-heavy index since April. The Russell 2000 (IWM) held almost flat, down just 0.5 percent from 298.18 to 296.69, suggesting the rotation away from large-cap growth and into defensives is real but not panicked.

The commodity complex was the epicenter. WTI crude (USO) fell 5.7 percent on the week; gold 4.9 percent; silver 12.1 percent. These are not correlated selloffs — they reflect the same underlying catalyst (Iran resolution removing war premium) hitting different risk profiles. The divergence between crude (down as supply reopens) and natural gas (UNG up 2.0 percent, near $11.73) suggests energy rotation rather than uniform demand destruction.

Internationally, the signal is worrying. EM equities (EEM) fell 5.6 percent from 71.21 to 67.25, erasing several months of gains. China large-cap (FXI) fell 3.2 percent (33.43 to 32.36). MSCI EAFE (EFA) fell a more modest 2.2 percent (104.58 to 102.26). The EM underperformance is not explained by Iran alone — dollar strength (UUP up 0.6 percent on the week) is compressing EM assets even as commodity importers should theoretically benefit from lower oil. The JPY remained weak at 161.74 to the dollar, offering no relief.

The most significant international equity signal came not from an ETF but from Moscow. The MOEX index fell 4.23 percent on June 22 to 2,318 points, its largest single-session decline since September 26, 2022 — the day after Putin announced partial mobilization. Gazprom shares fell below 100 rubles for the first time since November 2008. ALROSA fell 7.2 percent. The timing matters: this was the same day the US announced Iran oil sanctions relief, implying markets are already pricing Russian energy's loss of the war-scarcity premium that had been partially cushioning Kremlin finances.

## Politics and Policy: Washington and Elsewhere

The most consequential congressional action of the week was the Senate's 50-48 vote on June 23 to pass a war powers resolution directing the president to remove US forces from hostilities with Iran. Four Republicans voted with Democrats: Bill Cassidy (LA), Susan Collins (ME), Rand Paul (KY), and Lisa Murkowski (AK). This followed the House vote in early June (215-208, four Republicans). The resolution is the first war powers directive to pass both chambers in American history. It is not self-executing — it does not require the president's signature and carries no force of law absent a veto override — but the political signal is significant: GOP cohesion around executive war authority is cracking just as the administration is negotiating a ceasefire framework.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, enacted July 4, 2025) continued to cascade through the Federal Register this week. Key notices: BLM proposing to revise oil and gas leasing regulations to reflect OBBBA requirements; Interior adjusting royalties for onshore oil and gas lost from federal and Indian leases; USDA implementing changes to SNAP federal-state administrative cost sharing under Section 10106 of the Act; FHFA proposing to rescind its Duty to Serve Underserved Markets regulation; and OCC/FinCEN issuing AML rules for permitted payment stablecoin issuers. The breadth here is notable: a single statute enacted almost a year ago is still producing simultaneous rulemakings across a half-dozen agencies, compressing the normal implementation lag.

FEC fundraising data as of June 24 shows Democrats maintaining substantial cash advantages in contested Senate races. Jon Ossoff (GA) leads all candidates at $81.2 million raised in the 2026 cycle; James Talarico (TX) at $40.3 million; Cory Booker (NJ) at $32.3 million; S. Krishnamoorthi (IL) at $31.4 million; and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (House NY) at $31.1 million. Roy Cooper (NC) at $26.8 million has the strongest cash-on-hand ratio relative to disbursements. Graham (SC) is the most notable Republican, at $20.9 million raised but already net-negative at $-8.3 million after spending. The midterm map is shaping up as a significant financial asymmetry.

In New York on June 24, a Mamdani-backed slate swept three congressional primaries: Brad Lander defeated Rep. Dan Goldman in NY-10; Darializa Avila Chevalier, 32, defeated five-term incumbent Adriano Espaillat in NY-13; and Claire Valdez defeated Brooklyn Borough President Antonio Reynoso in NY-7. The results represent the clearest signal yet that Mayor Zohran Mamdani's democratic socialist movement is consolidating the New York City political base rather than simply holding it.

## Sanctions and Legal

The defining sanctions development of the week was the 60-day OFAC waiver on Iranian oil exports, announced June 22. The waiver covers production, delivery, and sale of Iranian crude, freeing an estimated 67 million barrels currently stored in tankers in the Gulf. Iran's foreign ministry said publicly that it made "no new commitments" on nuclear inspections, flatly contradicting the Vance characterization of the Swiss talks. The gap between what each side is claiming the deal means is itself a signal: this is a ceasefire, not a settlement.

The existing Russia sanctions database shows no significant new designations this week — the entries visible are legacy (May 2026 and earlier), covering Russian financial institutions including Belgazprombank, MIR Business Bank, Sberbank Europe, Tinkoff, and Sovcombank. The absence of fresh designations is consistent with the administration maintaining its ceasefire diplomatic posture across both Iran and Ukraine tracks simultaneously.

Court data from CourtListener shows routine circuit court activity with no major structural decisions visible in the dataset this week. The war powers resolution adds a layer to the existing legal ambiguity around the AUMF and executive authority in the Iran theater — but the constitutional question will not reach courts until Trump acts (or declines to act) on the resolution.

## Geopolitical Signal

The Iran theater dominated the geopolitical feed, but the specific shape of the resolution matters. The 60-day framework opens a negotiating window, not a closed deal. Polymarket has Benjamin Netanyahu entering Iran at 0 percent probability before June 30, and JD Vance at 0 percent — markets see the diplomatic track as durable at least through month-end. The Congress war powers vote adds domestic political pressure to conclude, not just pause. Rubio's public warning that no country can charge tolls for Strait of Hormuz transit was directed at Iran but also signals the floor condition: the US will not accept any Iranian leverage over commercial shipping even under a ceasefire.

On the Ukraine front, the week showed continued Ukrainian deep-strike capability. A drone attack on the Ufa oil refinery in the Urals was reported June 25 — a 1,600-km penetration. Ukraine also struck power infrastructure in Sevastopol, knocking out electricity in Russian-occupied Crimea. Russian forces continued pressure on Kostyantynivka in the Donbas, described as the last significant obstacle before Russian forces could push toward Pokrovsk. FIRMS thermal anomalies at coordinates (52.81, 32.23) and (52.85, 29.99) — the Sumy/Chernihiv direction — show consistent fire radiative power consistent with ongoing strikes.

The Meduza report on Russian mobilization planning is the most significant single piece of intelligence in this week's Russia feed. Sources inside the Presidential Administration say a new mobilization wave is being planned for October, sequenced after the September 18–20 State Duma elections. The mechanism would be newly elected deputies calling publicly for mobilization from the Duma floor, providing political cover. Contract recruitment has collapsed by more than a third year-over-year. The Kremlin denied any discussion of election postponement, but the internal debate is real.

In Latin America, Abelardo de la Espriella's victory in Colombia's June 21 runoff is the sharpest right-pivot in the hemisphere since Milei in Argentina. De la Espriella won 49.7 percent to Cepeda's 48.7 percent — a razor margin — but his platform is maximalist: end FARC peace talks, 90-day US-backed air campaign against armed groups, mega-prison construction, and full reversal of Petro's foreign policy including restoration of ties with Israel. He was Trump-endorsed and is a dual US-Colombian citizen who built his legal career defending clients including Alex Saab, Maduro's financier.

The Venezuela earthquake doublet of June 24 — M7.2 followed 38 seconds later by M7.5, both roughly 160 km west of Caracas — is the worst seismic event in Venezuela in more than a century. Confirmed deaths exceeded 235 by June 25, with more than 4,300 injured and casualty figures expected to rise significantly. The USGS PAGER system flagged possible fatalities in the tens of thousands. The Maduro government's capacity to manage disaster relief is severely constrained by the existing humanitarian crisis.

France confirmed its first Ebola case on June 24, a physician returning from a humanitarian mission in the DRC. The DRC outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain (for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment), has surpassed 1,048 confirmed cases and 267 deaths — the largest confirmed case count in the first month of any African Ebola outbreak on record. This is the first export of the current outbreak outside Africa.

## Cross-Section Themes

**1. The Iran premium deflation is a single cause with eight effects.** The ceasefire framework resolved simultaneously into: oil prices -37% from April peak; gold -5% on the week; silver -12%; MOEX -4.2% in one session; TLT +1.5%; Congress passing its first-ever war powers resolution; Polymarket pricing out Netanyahu/Vance entry into Iran; and France's VAT cut on family activities coming into force July 1 (a fiscal dividend the UK is explicitly taking as a political signal). One geopolitical development is restructuring energy, credit, FX, domestic politics, and legislative calendars simultaneously.

**2. Russia's dual crisis is accelerating.** The MOEX crash on June 22 (Gazprom below 100 rubles for the first time since 2008) and the Meduza mobilization reporting appear in different sections of the daily but they are the same story: the war's economic model is breaking down at exactly the moment the political system needs to hold together for September elections. Rosneft CEO Sechlin's request to Putin for emergency fuel market stabilization measures — reported in the Russian internal feed June 24 — adds a third signal. VK apps disappearing from the App Store on June 25 and Moscow mobile operators pre-warning of possible internet shutdowns on the same day suggest domestic information control is being tightened ahead of electoral pressure.

**3. The OBBBA regulatory cascade is the sleeper domestic story.** The Act enacted in July 2025 is still generating simultaneous rulemakings from BLM (oil/gas leasing), Interior (federal royalties), USDA (SNAP cost-sharing), FHFA (Duty to Serve rescission), and OCC/FinCEN (stablecoin AML). Almost every major regulatory agency is in active rulemaking under a single political mandate. The breadth of implementation is compressing normal administrative law timelines and creating an unusually target-rich environment for legal challenges.

**4. The Western hemisphere's political center is hollowing.** Within one week: UK loses its sixth PM in seven years; Colombia elects a far-right lawyer who defended a Maduro ally and promises to end peace talks; Venezuela's government faces its worst natural disaster in a century from a position of institutional collapse; and New York's congressional delegation is being remade by democratic socialists. None of these are causally connected, but they share a common substrate: the institutional center is not holding, in either direction.

**5. The Ebola-Bundibugyo export to France is a low-probability, high-consequence signal.** The specific strain matters: Bundibugyo has no approved vaccine (unlike Zaire strain, for which ring-vaccination protocols exist). The 1,048-case Congo outbreak surpassed first-month records before this export event. If French contact tracing holds, this remains a contained case; if it does not, the next three weeks determine whether this becomes a 2014-style international event. The timing — with France simultaneously managing a historic heatwave (40C+) and a political leadership vacuum — is a stress-test of public health infrastructure.
