WTI crude surged more than 6% to $97/barrel as Iran captured a US underwater drone, Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia intensified, and prediction markets priced Hormuz normalization at exactly 0% by Friday. ## Macro

The 10y-2y spread narrowed from 41bps to 39bps — still positive and no inversion signal — but the re-pricing is hawkish across the board.

The FOMC decision on **September 16** is now the week's most consequential near-term event. That move is not a minor fluctuation; at $97 WTI, refinery margin pressure and fuel-driven consumer inflation return as live policy concerns. The cause is analyzed in the geopolitical section.

VIX crept from **14.32** to **16.46** through Thursday — subdued by historical standards but a clear directional signal. USD/JPY moved from 159.97 to **154.25** over the week, a yen strengthening of roughly 5.7 big figures — consistent with flight-to-quality flows and perhaps anticipation of Bank of Japan positioning relative to a more hawkish Fed.

## Markets

US equities extended their pullback throughout the week. **SPY** (S&P 500 ETF) moved from a 770.19 prior-Friday close to 765.96 Monday, 762.40 Tuesday, and 757.83 Wednesday — roughly **-1.6% through mid-week**. Technology led the rotation lower: **QQQ** (Nasdaq-100) posted a -1.06% session on Wednesday, retreating to 708.69. **IWM** (Russell 2000 small-caps) sustained the steepest domestic decline, falling from 296.01 to 287.70 by Wednesday — small-caps bearing the brunt of rate-hike re-pricing.

International markets compounded the weakness. **EFA** (MSCI EAFE developed markets) fell from 108.35 to 105.66 across the week. **EEM** (MSCI Emerging Markets) dropped from 68.70 to **67.00** by Wednesday, with a -2.16% single-session move — the week's worst equity result. Emerging market assets are structurally exposed to a combination of USD strength, crude cost-push, and tightening global liquidity.

One data point distorted the billionaire wealth picture: **Elon Musk** jumped from $908B (Monday) to $942B (Wednesday), adding approximately $34B in two sessions. Commentary flagged this as a market response to "AGI news" — a theme echoed in the MargRev Friday post ("Markets do respond to AGI news"). The episodic nature of these moves — massive single-stock/single-figure swings tied to AI developments — signals that AGI-adjacent announcements are now priced by institutional markets in real time.

## Geopolitical Signal: Iran Escalation Cascade

The week's most structurally significant story is not any single headline but a coherent **Iran escalation cascade** across five independent signals:

1. **OFAC density:** Four Iran-related actions in five business days — designations, General License modifications, and a new GL DD authorization. This pace is unusual and operationally significant.
2. **Strait of Hormuz:** Polymarket's "Strait of Hormuz traffic returns to normal by September 15" contract closed the week at **0% YES** — the market sees no normalization scenario in this time window.
3. **US invasion market:** "US invades Iran before 2027" at **16%** — elevated but not dominant; consistent with a coercive posture short of kinetic action.
4. **Iran captures US drone (Sept 9):** Iran claimed capture of a US underwater drone/submarine. The US characterized it as a malfunction. This is a provocation/intelligence operation that, regardless of who framed it accurately, raises diplomatic temperature.
5. **Houthi-Saudi escalation (Sept 10):** Pakistan delivered a Saudi warning to Iran regarding Houthi attacks. Houthis simultaneously intensified strikes on Saudi Arabia. Pakistan as intermediary is geopolitically unusual and suggests Saudi Arabia is seeking back-channel pressure.

These five signals — sanctions pace, market pricing, drone incident, Houthi escalation, and Pakistan as Saudi messenger — are mutually reinforcing. The WTI crude spike from $91 to $97 is the market's integrated read on this cluster.
