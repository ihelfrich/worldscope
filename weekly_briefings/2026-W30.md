## Headline of the week

The fragile US-Iran ceasefire that had held since mid-June collapsed in the first week of July, and by mid-week of W30 the war had widened into its most dangerous phase yet. Brent crude crossed $100 per barrel on Thursday -- a 40-percent surge from $71.57 on July 1 -- as Iranian-backed Houthis attacked Saudi tankers in the Red Sea, the IRGC escalated strikes on US military bases across the Gulf, and the Strait of Hormuz remained functionally closed. On the same day equity markets fell, oil volatility futures jumped 4.5 percent, gold sold off (a sign the market is pricing inflation, not safety), and the White House unveiled a third tariff architecture in as many months. Those three simultaneous shocks -- energy war, trade-policy instability, and rising inflation expectations -- define the regime entering the second half of 2026.

## Macro: how the picture moved

The central macro fact of the week is that the Iran war has broken through into realized inflation and is now the dominant constraint on central bank action in both Washington and Frankfurt.

**Rates and curve:** The Federal funds effective rate sits at 3.63 percent. The 2-year Treasury closed the week at 4.31 percent, the 10-year at 4.67 percent, the 30-year at 5.15 percent. The 10Y-2Y spread is +34 basis points -- positively sloped but barely, a curve that reflects priced-in "higher for longer" rather than confident easing. The Fed meets July 28-29. Prediction markets on Thursday gave 74 percent odds to no change and 25 percent odds to a 25-basis-point hike, with cut probability essentially zero. Governor Waller titled his July 13 speech "Monetary Policy at a Crossroads"; that captures the bind exactly. War-driven oil is inflationary, but it is also a demand shock if it sustains. The Committee cannot cut into $100 oil without credibility damage, and cannot hike into slowing growth without recession risk.

**Oil:** The FRED WTI series showed $84.38 as of July 20 (data lag); real-time prices by Thursday July 23 were $92.19 WTI and $100.69 Brent, the first Brent triple-digit print in two months. Polymarket gives a 1-percent probability that Hormuz traffic returns to normal by July 31. That is the market's clean statement: this is not a spike, it is a blockade repricing.

**ECB:** The Governing Council held at 2.25 percent on July 23, explicitly citing the Iran war's uncertain inflationary pass-through. Bloomberg reported that Lagarde paused specifically to gauge war risks. The ECB raised its deposit rate by 25 basis points in June; the September 10 meeting is now priced for another hike if energy prices stay elevated. The ECB also published its Q3 Survey of Professional Forecasters and consumer expectations data the same day.

**Russia:** The Bank of Russia cut its key rate for the tenth consecutive meeting, by 25 basis points to 14 percent, in a decision that surprised most analysts who had expected a hold. The CBR simultaneously cut its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 0-1 percent (from 0.5-1.5 percent) and raised its inflation forecast to 6-7 percent (from 4.5-5.5 percent), citing "considerable rise in fuel prices." The paradox: higher global oil prices are revenue-positive for the Russian budget at the moment but are materializing inside Russia as domestic fuel inflation that is eroding real wages. The ruble sits at 78.4 per dollar.

**Dollar and EM:** EUR/USD at 1.144 per the FRED lag; real-time the dollar strengthened modestly on Thursday (UUP +0.39 percent). Asian EM currencies broadly stable: USD/CNY 6.78, USD/KRW 1,474, USD/TWD 32.4. The Japanese yen at 163.7 per dollar remains under notable pressure.

## Markets: rotation and dispersion

Thursday's session crystallized the week's positioning logic. SPY fell 1.23 percent, QQQ fell 1.90 percent -- tech took the larger hit -- while small caps (IWM) fell only 0.58 percent and Chinese large-caps (FXI) actually gained 0.09 percent. That divergence is signal: the market is pricing China as partially insulated from Hormuz, and small caps as partially insulated from tech-led multiple compression.

The commodity complex told a clearer story. USO (WTI crude) was up 5.93 percent Thursday alone. Natural gas (UNG) was up 0.09 percent -- the war premium has not yet spread to gas, which is a secondary flag for European buyers. Agriculture (DBA) was nearly flat. Gold (GLD) fell 2.00 percent and silver (SLV) fell 3.45 percent. This is unusual: precious metals declining during active military conflict typically means markets are pricing inflationary war, not deflationary fear. Real rates are rising as nominal yields move up, compressing gold.

Credit spreads widened modestly: HYG fell 0.36 percent, LQD fell 0.38 percent. The spread between high yield and investment grade did not blow out, which means the credit market is not yet pricing a recession from this oil shock -- consistent with the Fed's "crossroads" framing.

Crypto sold off: Bitcoin futures (BITO) fell 2.01 percent Thursday, BTC spot at $64,987, ETH at $1,881 (down 2.03 percent), SOL at $75.14 (down 3.12 percent). Crypto is behaving as a risk asset this week, not a hedge.

The weekly trend data shows the Ukraine Theater feed at 290 items Friday versus a 7-day median of 443 -- the count has fallen as coverage concentrated on the Iran story, but the events have not slowed (see below). Russian internal news was at 1,138 items Friday versus a 7-day median of 982, the highest volume of the week, reflecting domestic coverage of the CBR decision, Wildberries strikes, and the Kyiv attack.

## Politics and policy: what moved in Washington and elsewhere

The dominant Washington story of the week is tariff-regime sequencing. The Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling that emergency powers do not authorize presidential tariffs forced the administration to use Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 as a fallback, imposing 10-percent worldwide tariffs. Section 122 has a 150-day limit; that limit expires this week. Thursday's announcement of new tariffs of 10 to 12.5 percent on more than 60 countries -- covering 99 percent of US imports by value -- invokes a forced-labor authority rather than emergency powers. PIIE analysts wrote within hours that these too are likely to be struck down by the Supreme Court. The administration is aware; it is cycling through statutory authorities faster than courts can rule.

The Federal Register spiked on July 23 with 41 new documents, nearly double the 7-day median of 22. The July 24 count (32) remains elevated. The carrying terms this week include "regulatory," "proposing," "safety," and "guard" -- consistent with a regulatory burst coinciding with the tariff rollout and attendant agency actions.

The FinCEN extension of comment period for its Huione Group proposed rule (July 24 Federal Register) is worth flagging separately: the portal failure that forced the extension suggests unusually heavy public comment interest in the crypto-adjacent money laundering designation, a sign that the enforcement perimeter around digital financial infrastructure is under scrutiny.

On the political calendar: Connecticut is running a competitive primary between Larson and Bronin with advertising in the final weeks. Arizona's gubernatorial contest features Andy Biggs, whose healthcare and abortion record is drawing attention from the medical community. The cyclosporiasis outbreak (4,173 confirmed cases across 41 states, doubled in one week) has emerged as a public health issue that may affect the political environment in food-safety-sensitive districts.

In Poland, more than 30 members of Law and Justice (PiS) resigned from the party over a conflict involving Morawiecki's new coalition initiative. This is a meaningful fracture in the largest opposition party in the EU's sixth-largest economy.

## Geopolitical signal

The Iran story is now multi-threaded and accelerating. The sequence: US resumed strikes after Iran hit Gulf shipping on July 6-7. Iran escalated by striking US military facilities in Qatar, Kuwait, and the UAE. The US completed three consecutive nights of strikes against Iranian assets in the week of July 13-17. By July 23, the conflict had expanded to include Houthi attacks on Saudi tankers in the Red Sea -- widening the maritime chokepoint risk from one strait (Hormuz) to two (Hormuz plus Bab el-Mandeb). The local news feed shows drone attacks on Irbil (Iraq), where US forces are based. The prediction market signal is unusually clear: 1 percent odds for Hormuz normalization by July 31 is effectively a consensus that the blockade continues through month-end.

The Iran succession question is the structural bet running underneath the tactical war. Polymarket prices Mojtaba Khamenei -- Ali Khamenei's son -- at 72 percent probability to be head of state by end of 2026. The same market gives 30 percent odds to a US invasion of Iran before 2027. Those two forecasts are related but not identical: the US could install conditions that accelerate succession without a formal invasion. The ceasefire between Israel and Iran (separately from the US-Iran conflict) held at 74 percent odds of surviving through July 25 as of Thursday.

Ukraine theater: Russia struck a private military training range near Kyiv on July 24, killing 10 and wounding approximately 100. The facility was linked to the founder of First Contact, a Ukrainian defense producer. Ukraine's SBU responded the same night with long-range strikes on Russian oil refineries, fuel infrastructure, and radar stations in Russia and occupied Crimea. Ukraine has been hitting Wildberries logistics centers near-daily since July 18; Russian analysts estimate damages at up to 100 billion rubles ($1.3 billion). Crimea's electricity consumption fell 42 percent in the first 20 days of July versus the same period last year. Putin made an unannounced visit to the Irkutsk aviation factory on July 24 -- only the fourth city he has visited outside Moscow this year, and the choice of a military aviation plant is not incidental.

VIP military flight tracking shows two anomalous spikes this week: July 22 and July 24 each logged 30 government/military aircraft vs a baseline of 8-9. Bulgarian military aircraft (JAF50T, JAF5GA) appeared over Spain and North Africa -- an unusual routing for a NATO member's air force.

European wildfires are severe: tens of thousands were evacuated from France's Cap Ferret peninsula (a major tourist destination) and areas near Madrid. The GDACS global disaster alert count rose from 101 on July 17 to 246 on July 24 -- a 144 percent increase in one week, driven by Tropical Storm Bertha's Gulf Coast impact (GDELT GKG flagged "Tropical Storm Bertha weakens after bringing strong winds and heavy surf to Louisiana and Texas"), flood watches across central Alabama and northern Georgia, and wildfire events in Western Europe.

## Cross-section themes (the synthesis layer)

**1. Iran as the binding macro variable.** The same event -- the Hormuz blockade and US-Iran war re-escalation -- appears simultaneously in the oil market (+40 percent in three weeks), equity markets (tech-led sell-off), ECB rate decision (explicit hold-to-gauge rationale), Fed market pricing (hike odds rising), Russian CBR inflation forecast upgrade, shipping lanes (Red Sea plus Hormuz), and VIP flight anomalies. When a single event appears in six independent data feeds simultaneously, it is the week's signal. Everything else is noise relative to this.

**2. The tariff-court-tariff cycle as structural instability.** This is the third distinct tariff architecture in twelve months: IEEPA emergency tariffs (struck down by SCOTUS in February), Section 122 worldwide tariffs (expiring this week), and now forced-labor authority tariffs on 60-plus countries. PIIE's immediate analysis that the new regime also faces strong legal headwinds means businesses cannot price these tariffs as durable. The investment uncertainty created by the cycling through of authorities may be more economically damaging than any specific tariff level.

**3. Ukraine's second-order economic war.** Ukraine is no longer limiting its strikes to military hardware and oil depots. Targeting Wildberries -- Russia's largest e-commerce platform -- signals a deliberate attempt to degrade civilian supply chains and impose visible consumer costs on the Russian population. The $1.3 billion estimated damage figure, combined with the 42-percent drop in Crimean electricity consumption and the CBR's lowered GDP forecast, suggests the economic pressure is cumulative and accelerating.

**4. Dual chokepoint risk.** The Iran war has now opened threats to both the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb simultaneously (the latter via Houthi attacks on Saudi tankers). These are the first and third most important maritime chokepoints for global energy trade. The Conversable Economist piece noted that Suez, Malacca, and the Panama Canal are the other major chokepoints. Shipping companies are now stress-testing routes around all of them simultaneously.

**5. Succession risk in Iran as a priced uncertainty.** The 72-percent Polymarket odds on Mojtaba Khamenei as head of state by year-end, combined with the 30-percent invasion probability, constitute the market's read that the current Iranian government structure is in flux. Supreme Leader succession has not occurred in Iran since 1989. The market is pricing a non-trivial probability of fundamental regime change -- which would have implications for sanctions structure, oil flow restoration, and the entire architecture of the Abraham Accords framework.
