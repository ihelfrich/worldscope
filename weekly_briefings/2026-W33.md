# Weekly brief — 2026-W33 (August 9–14, 2026)

> **Note:** Weekly bundle for 2026-W33 was not yet available at publication time; this brief draws from the August 14 daily bundle plus five prior daily bundles (August 9–13) for cross-day trajectory. FALLBACK=1.

## Headline of the week

The Iran war's fifth month produced its clearest economic convergence yet: when Ukraine struck Russia's Novorossiysk grain terminals and Russia retaliated against Ukraine's Danube port, the feedback loop between the Middle East oil shock and Black Sea food disruption locked in hard. Grain futures spiked, crude oil rose nearly 8% on the week, and the Federal Reserve — already five holds into a contested pause — found its September optionality contracting. Against that macro backdrop, a NATO jet shot down a stray drone over Latvia (the second such intercept in two months), the USS Abraham Lincoln's mental health crisis became a measure of what 260 days of sustained carrier operations in a contested strait actually costs the force, and Trump signed a proclamation imposing 100% tariffs on imported drones, framing the emerging drone economy as a national security perimeter.

## Macro: how the picture moved

The 10-year Treasury opened the week at 4.69%, rose to 4.72% mid-week on CPI day and renewed oil-market pressure, then settled to 4.68%. The 2-year Treasury tracked in a 4.19%–4.25% range. The 10y–2y spread widened slightly to +48bps, maintaining the positive curve that returned earlier this year for the first time since the 2022–2023 inversion. At 3.63% SOFR and 3.63% effective fed funds, real short rates are positive but only modestly so against a 3.4% headline.

July CPI printed at 3.4%, down one tick from June's 3.5%, on a monthly increase of 0.1%. The directional movement is favorable; the composition is not. Gasoline prices were +24.6% year-over-year (down from 26.7% in June), fuel oil +39.1%, both driven by the Hormuz closure that began in late February. Shelter contributed approximately two-thirds of the monthly headline increase. Core CPI also ticked down but remains structurally elevated.

The Fed held for the fifth consecutive meeting at its July gathering, with Chair Warsh framing the pause as a high-bar hold rather than capitulation. Three dissenting members voted to hike immediately. As of Thursday's prediction-market pricing: 66% probability of a hold at the September 16 meeting, 32% probability of a 25bps hike, 1% probability of a cut. The crude trajectory from this week — WTI proxy (USO) rising from 117.98 on August 9 to 127.30 on August 13, a 7.9% gain in five trading sessions driven by the grain hub attacks and fresh Iranian tanker targeting — means if oil holds here or moves higher, the September hike probability migrates into the 40%–50% range.

The yen continued its weakening path, sitting at 159.39 per dollar against 157.54 the prior week, a move consistent with the US-Japan rate differential persisting. EUR/USD softened marginally. The dollar (DXY proxy UUP) ticked from 28.07 to 28.18–28.20. Gold held the 398–405 range without breaking to fresh highs.

## Markets: rotation and dispersion

The week's headline was crude oil, but the structural story was the gap between large-cap tech and everything else. QQQ (Nasdaq 100) gained +1.2% on the week to 732.07; SPY (S&P 500) gained +0.6% to 777.88; IWM (Russell 2000) gained only +0.7% to 303.50 after being meaningfully underwater mid-week. The QQQ premium reflects AI-related earnings momentum; the IWM underperformance reflects rate sensitivity and regional bank exposure. These two animals are living very different 2026s.

China large-cap (FXI) fell approximately 3.6% on the week, from 36.17 to 34.86, underperforming every major regional equity index. The Futu/Tiger options insider-trading case (45 individuals charged, $155M in combined illicit profits) landed alongside a reported sharp slowdown in Tencent's profit growth, JD.com revenue declining as state rebate programs wind down, and ongoing property-sector malaise. FXI, however, captures only part of the Chinese tech picture: SMIC reported Q2 net profit up 217.5% year-over-year on AI chip-adjacent NAND demand and confirmed its position as the third-largest global NAND flash supplier; China's new-energy vehicle market share topped 60% for the first time in July; and Apple confirmed it built a China-specific large-language model in partnership with Alibaba. The large-cap index decline and the underlying technology acceleration are running simultaneously.

Silver outperformed gold significantly on the week (+2.7%, from 57.50 to 59.06), its typical behavior in mid-cycle phases where industrial demand combines with safe-haven flows. Agriculture (DBA) gained +0.8% as grain-hub attack news rippled through futures markets. VIX futures (VXX) declined from 20.32 to 19.45–19.62 over the week — a counterintuitive move given geopolitical intensity, explained by the tech equity bid suppressing measured implied volatility. Credit spreads remained broadly contained: HYG +0.23% on the week, LQD +0.41%, suggesting financial conditions have not materially tightened despite the rates uncertainty.

The week's only real macro anomaly was the oil-agriculture correlation tightening: two separate supply-shock vectors (Hormuz crude, Black Sea grain) moving in the same direction in the same week, validating the scenario that commodity markets priced as a tail risk at the start of the Iran War.

## Politics and policy: what moved in Washington and elsewhere

The Federal Register ran at or below its 7-day median this week (18–20 items per day versus a spike to 31 on August 11 and 29 on August 12), indicating a summer regulatory deceleration. The substantive exception was a CMS final rule prohibiting Medicaid funding for gender-affirming procedures for minors — a culture-war rulemaking with federal-funding leverage that will generate Tenth Amendment litigation.

The significant policy action was executive: Trump signed a proclamation imposing 100% tariffs on heavy/sensitive drones and components, 25% on smaller drones and other parts. Ally differentiation was explicit: EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein at 15%; UK at 10%. The three-week phase-in and six-month delay for non-security-sensitive components are designed to cushion near-term supply chain disruption while establishing the domestic-manufacturing narrative. The motivation is legible from this week's Ukraine theater data: Russia launched 100–200 strike drones per day against Ukrainian cities, North Korean ballistic missiles have been confirmed striking Zaporizhzhia, and the drone logistics chain has become the backbone of modern near-peer attrition warfare. Washington is now treating drone industrial capacity as a first-tier national security variable, on par with semiconductors.

The FEC data is the most structurally significant political signal of the week. Jon Ossoff (D-GA, Senate) has raised $97.99M in the 2026 cycle — a total that would have been implausible for a Senate race under any prior financing regime. James Talarico holds $68.56M for his Texas Senate run; Sherrod Brown $38.57M for Ohio; Roy Cooper $34.98M for North Carolina; Cory Booker $33.50M for New Jersey; Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez $32.61M for her House seat. That last number is anomalous for a safe seat; it reflects resource accumulation for a higher-office bid. Democrats hold a combined $238M Senate fundraising advantage over Republicans cycle-to-date. Lindsey Graham has disbursed more than he has raised, an unusual cash-flow pattern for a senior incumbent.

Reports circulated that Karoline Leavitt is evaluating her tenure as White House Press Secretary, with the question of succession now live.

## Sanctions and legal

The week's sanctions feed was dominated by legacy Russian financial-sector entries dated April–May 2026 (Tinkoff Bank, Sovcombank, Novikombank, and similar) rather than new designations — a signal that the administration's sanctions production cadence has paused during the August recess. New entry velocity is a leading indicator of pressure escalation; the absence of new designations in a week when Ukraine struck Russian territory significantly is worth tracking.

The Court of International Trade saw ADM file twice against US tariff actions, signaling that the agricultural sector's litigation against import duties is intensifying precisely as grain prices are being driven higher from the supply side by Black Sea attacks. The combined pressure — tariff litigation disrupting US agricultural exports, Black Sea attacks disrupting Russian and Ukrainian grain exports — is narrowing the global supply margin.

The Fifth Circuit produced a ruling in Citizens for Clean Air v. TRAN (transportation-sector clean air challenge), and the Ninth Circuit handled the Devas Multimedia arbitration appeal against Antrix Corporation. Neither is immediately significant, but the Devas case is worth watching as a bellwether for sovereign arbitration enforcement against state-owned commercial entities.

## Geopolitical signal

The Strait of Hormuz logged just 3 transits in the 24 hours ending August 12, compared to a 14-transit peak on August 11 and a pre-war daily average of approximately 138 vessels. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps announced it targeted two tankers attempting to transit with US air support. On Liveuamap, Iran declared that the strait will not return to pre-war operating conditions, asserting that only routes agreed upon with Oman and specific partner states will be authorized. Prediction markets price Hormuz normalization by August 31 at 3%, and by August 15 at 0%. That declaration — not the tanker targeting itself — is the week's most important geopolitical statement. It signals that Iran is treating Hormuz access as a permanent bargaining chip rather than a temporary tactical tool.

On the eastern NATO flank, Italian Eurofighters based in Lithuania scrambled Friday morning and downed a drone over Latvia near the Russian border, with Turkish F-16s from Estonia also launched. Latvia attributed the incident to Russian electromagnetic warfare redirecting the UAV into its airspace. This is the second NATO intercept over Latvia in two months, following incidents in Bulgaria, Romania, and a stray Russian missile that landed in Poland. Each incident is contained; the cumulative pattern is that the eastern flank is experiencing semi-regularized airspace violations at a pace that is normalizing what would have been a significant crisis two years ago.

The DPRK-Russia military nexus deepened materially. North Korean ballistic missiles have now been confirmed in strikes on Zaporizhzhia, per Ukrainian open-source reporting this week. The relationship has moved past weapons supply into operational fire-mission integration.

Putin made his first-ever visit to the Kuril Islands. Japan protested as "absolutely unacceptable." The timing — US carrier forces at maximum operational stress, Washington bandwidth consumed by the Iran War — is consistent with Moscow's established pattern of testing territorial assertions when great-power attention is distributed.

The West Bank situation produced the week's most politically anomalous signal. US Ambassador Mike Huckabee, a figure who has consistently supported Israeli settlement activity, called the settler siege of Palestinian homes in Qusra a "horrific act of terror." The White House is pushing Netanyahu to publicly condemn the incident. A Palestinian-American home is among those under siege. The intensity of the rebuke from a pro-settler ambassador is a potential indicator of a shift in administration posture — or of the limits of that shift, since settlement construction approvals have continued in parallel. Eighteen Palestinians have been killed in settler attacks in 2026 as of late July; the UN has characterized West Bank settler violence as at an "all-time high."

The USS Abraham Lincoln, 260 days into one of the longest carrier deployments in modern history, registered a sailor-overboard mental health episode (the sailor was recovered and uninjured). Families of roughly 5,000 crew members met with senior Navy leaders in San Diego. Food and water shortages have been reported. A replacement carrier is being dispatched. Defense Secretary Hegseth publicly disputed the framing, but the incident has generated Congressional inquiries. This is not a marginal story: it is the human-capital cost of sustained carrier operations in the Hormuz corridor made visible.

VIP flight data from August 13 showed German military aircraft (GAF230) tracking over Israeli territory at 7,178m, US PUMA71 over central Spain, and a US NCR831 aircraft over the Taiwan Strait — a positional snapshot consistent with the week's broader geopolitical tempo.

## Cross-section themes: the synthesis layer

**Theme 1: The Iran War's supply chain tentacles are longer than the map implies.** The Hormuz closure affects LPG flows to Asia, fertilizer imports across the Indo-Pacific, and the availability of petrochemical feedstocks from Gulf refineries. The USO weekly +8% move reflects not just crude but a repricing of every commodity that touches the Hormuz-Red Sea corridor. Add Black Sea grain hub attacks, and two of the three primary global commodity export corridors are simultaneously disrupted. The European drought shutting down Romania's Cernavoda nuclear plant (which supplies 20% of Romanian electricity) because the Danube is too low to provide cooling water is the third leg: the climate-geopolitical-commodity triad is tightening simultaneously.

**Theme 2: Drones are the defining object of the current geopolitical moment.** Trump's tariff proclamation, NATO's Latvia intercept, DPRK missiles in Zaporizhzhia, Ukraine's strike on Novorossiysk (drone-enabled), Russia's daily attrition campaign, Germany considering offensive cyberattacks on Russian drone manufacturers, the US attempting to restrict a domestic drone firm it alleges has DJI ties, and Wildberries warehouses burned by Ukrainian drones. These are not separate stories. The economic, legal, and military architecture around drone production and employment is being built in real time while drones are simultaneously being deployed at industrial scale.

**Theme 3: The Fed's dilemma has a structural driver the rate instrument cannot reach.** Shelter contributing two-thirds of monthly CPI is a supply-side housing problem that monetary policy addresses only through demand destruction severe enough to collapse construction employment. The energy shock from Hormuz is supply-side and geopolitical. The Fed can tighten into both — and the market is pricing a ~32% chance it does so in September — but neither variable responds to the federal funds rate in the near term. Warsh has framed the pause as high-conviction; the commodity data this week erodes that framing.

**Theme 4: American Democratic fundraising is operating at a scale that suggests genuine expectation of a wave environment.** Ossoff at $97.99M for a Georgia Senate seat, Talarico at $68.56M for Texas, AOC at $32.61M for a safe House seat — these are not normal midterm figures. Combined with the broader $238M Democratic Senate advantage, the money suggests the donor class has internalized the Iran War's political costs, the Lincoln carrier crisis, and West Bank friction with the Trump administration as durable political liabilities heading into November.

**Theme 5: Chinese technology is bifurcating between macro headwinds and structural acceleration.** FXI declining 3.6% on the week; SMIC netting 217.5% profit growth. NEV penetration crossing 60%; JD.com revenue declining as state subsidies roll off. Apple building a China-specific LLM with Alibaba; Chinese sovereign funds appearing in SpaceX shareholders. The US-China technology relationship in 2026 is not decoupling — it is a contested dual-track relationship where the separation narrative and the integration reality coexist and contradict each other across different layers of the stack.
