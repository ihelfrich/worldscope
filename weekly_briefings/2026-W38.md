## Headline of the week

The United States government ran three simultaneous and contradictory Russia policies in the same week. Congress passed the Lindsey Graham sanctions bill -- the so-called "hell sanctions" targeting Russian oil and gas exports -- while the White House signaled that Trump intends to pursue business deals with the Kremlin before the war ends, and OFAC simultaneously issued Russia-related designation removals (Sept 16). Those three threads, combined with a Federal Reserve that held rates while the 10-year Treasury crossed 5.00 percent, a Trump-brokered Ukraine-Russia energy truce that neither side honored, an EU-Canada institutional breakthrough in Strasbourg, and a Thursday Federal Register that repealed greenhouse gas emission standards and launched a "revolutionary" rewrite of federal procurement rules, make W38 the week where the contradictions of this political moment stopped being latent and became legible in real time.

## Macro: how the picture moved

The FOMC issued its September 16 statement following the two-day meeting and held the federal funds rate unchanged. The effective rate remained at 3.63 percent all week. Context matters: in W36, prediction markets had priced a 50/50 chance of a 25 basis point hike at this exact meeting; the hold was not the baseline. Markets initially absorbed the decision with modest relief but then repriced the longer end upward through the session: the 10-year Treasury closed the week at 5.00 percent, up from 4.95 percent at the start, and the 2-year moved from 4.56 percent to 4.67 percent. SOFR ended at 3.62 percent. The 10/2 spread narrowed from 0.33 to 0.27 percentage points, a mild flattening signal.

This is the second consecutive week where the long end has pushed higher while the Fed held. In W36 the 10-year stood at 4.79 percent; it is now 21 basis points higher in a fortnight, and the 5.00 percent handle is a level that last exerted visible pressure on equity multiples in late 2023. The Fed's decision appeared less about the labor market (unemployment 4.1 percent as of August, the last available print) and more about the continuing energy-price pass-through from the US-Iran conflict. Kevin Warsh's FOMC, like its predecessor, seems to be in a "watch and hold" stance -- not willing to tighten into an oil shock but unwilling to cut while the long end drifts higher.

Oil (USO) was the week's most volatile instrument. It rose sharply Monday and Wednesday, touched its weekly high mid-week, then fell more than 3.5 percent Thursday and gave up another 0.6 percent Friday to close at $155.31. The spike echoed Sunday's flash point: an Iranian commercial vessel was struck near Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, killing one crew member. The retracement by week's end suggests the market initially overcounted the incident as a prelude to direct US-Iranian naval exchange and then revised. Gold (GLD) ran counter to the pattern, ending the week at $398.36 after a strong Friday (+1.69 percent), suggesting some late-week risk-off positioning. The DXY proxy (UUP) firmed slightly across the week from $28.22 to $28.38.

The headline macro read: a hawkish hold. Rates stay higher for longer, the curve presses toward flat, oil shocks remain the wild card, and the 5 percent 10-year will need to be absorbed by equity multiples in coming weeks.

## Markets: rotation and dispersion

Risk assets sold off Mon-Thu and then recovered Friday, leaving most indices essentially flat for the week. SPY closed Friday at 762.60 (+1.13 percent on the day) after touching intraweek lows near 754, a net weekly move of roughly -0.2 percent from Sunday's 764.29 close. QQQ finished at 716.92 (weekly gain of about +0.3 percent), outperforming large-cap on Friday's session (+1.73 percent), a pattern consistent with the market reading the rate hold as tech-friendly at the margin. The divergence between tech and the rest was visible in small-cap underperformance: IWM lost 1.2 percent for the week (285.43 from 288.89), and EM (EEM) fell 1.4 percent (66.91 from 67.84). China large-cap (FXI) also lost 0.9 percent.

The dispersion is telling. Tech/Nasdaq recovered faster than cyclicals and EM, which are more sensitive to the energy pass-through and the dollar's modest strengthening. Within EM, the week's move was probably amplified by two related factors: the Graham Act sanctions adding a new risk premium to any EM exposure with significant Russia or Russia-adjacent energy ties, and the Hormuz/Bab el-Mandeb tension which disproportionately affects Asian importers.

Gold's Friday spike and the modest dollar gain are the two instruments that best encode the week's signal: a "hold but not-so-dovish" FOMC amid geopolitical noise. The fact that Friday was a broad-based relief rally after the midweek selloff -- rather than a fundamental re-rating -- means the regime question stays open heading into October.

## Politics and policy: what moved in Washington and elsewhere

The Federal Register was unusually active this week. The headline is Friday's simultaneous publication of multiple "Revolutionary FAR Overhaul" rules -- Parts 8/12/13/15/38/44/51 and Parts 16/17 and Parts 9/27 and Parts 14/28 published in the same edition, a coordinated overhaul of the Federal Acquisition Regulation governing essentially all federal procurement. The scale is unusual; sweeping FAR rewrites are generally phased. Also Friday: ITAR modifications removing certain uncrewed underwater vehicles (UUVs) from the US Munitions List Category XX(a), an ITAR policy clarification updating "Major Non-NATO Ally" designations, and OPM's proposed rule amending excepted service regulations and administrative law judge appointments (the latter directly relevant to Schedule F-adjacent civil service restructuring).

Thursday (Sept 17) brought two EPA actions that collectively represent the most significant environmental deregulation of the year: rescission of the greenhouse gas findings for fossil fuel-fired power plants and a partial repeal of the carbon pollution standards for electric generating units. These actions, combined with Monday's Federal Register entries expanding and adjusting the scope of Canadian tariff exclusions (the US-Canada trade dispute continuing to generate regulatory churn), and the Friday Presidential Determination on Major Drug Transit Countries for FY2027, suggest a pace of regulatory transformation that exceeds what the daily brief can easily absorb.

Wednesday (Sept 16): a DOJ press release on "Liability for Unregistered Foreign Agents" landed in the FARA feed, the same day the administration revealed the "RIS Network" -- a Russian intelligence-linked criminal network that had been recruiting for assassinations of Russian opposition figures and sabotage in Ukraine-allied countries. Among the accused: a retired colonel described as having worked directly with Putin and his son, described as an FSB employee. The FARA action the same day as the assassination network reveal is either coincidence or a signal of coordinated enforcement pressure on foreign influence operations.

California was the busiest state this week on the regulatory front: Newsom signed the AI-generated advertising disclosure law (Sept 16), the wildfire recovery package, the zero-emissions ferry executive action, and the lowrider/classic car preservation legislation. California's legislative session ended with a burst of Newsom signatures that position the state as a counterweight to federal deregulation -- particularly notable given the EPA's GHG rescissions arriving the same week.

The Russian Duma elections began today (September 18) and run through September 20. Three-day elections in authoritarian systems exist precisely to manage turnout and produce results. This week's run-up was notable: the Navalny Foundation (FBK) and opposition strategist Maxim Katz published a rare joint voting recommendation against United Russia on Monday, their first public coordination. The Kremlin's response was atmospheric rather than overt -- hockey star Alexander Ovechkin appeared in a United Russia campaign ad, and a Greens party Duma candidate (Yulia Kuznetsova) went missing the day before voting began.

## Sanctions and legal

OFAC was active in both directions. September 14: a new Iran-related designation, plus Venezuela General License No. 52C. September 16: Russia-related designation removals alongside counter-narcotics removal and Venezuela GL No. 5Z. September 17: Iran-related and Cuba designations alongside Belarus-related removals. The simultaneous addition and removal across Iran/Russia/Venezuela/Belarus is not unusual for OFAC's routine maintenance -- but the timing of Russia-related removals on the day the Graham Act passed through Congress, if that timing is confirmed, creates a politically legible contrast.

The FARA enforcement press release (Sept 16) and the RIS Network announcement the same day suggest the DOJ is treating foreign agent accountability and physical threat interdiction as part of a unified foreign influence disruption posture. The assassination network reportedly targeted Russian opposition figures in Washington and Vilnius -- the Vilnius dimension implicates NATO Article 5 terrain.

On the courts docket: the 2nd Circuit, 5th Circuit, 11th Circuit, and D.C. Circuit all posted opinions this week (per the courtlistener feed). The 2nd Circuit case involving a tax partnership dispute (Soroban Capital Partners LP v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue) may be worth tracking for carried-interest and partnership taxation implications. The courtlistener JSON did not return party names on individual cases in the extracted format, so analysis is limited to circuit-level observation.

EU sanctions against Putin and Lavrov expired or came up for renewal this week with the Coreper (Committee of Permanent Representatives) unable to agree, pushing the next meeting to Monday, September 21. This is a meaningful signal of internal EU cohesion strain -- unanimity required for sanctions renewal and at least one member blocking.

## Geopolitical signal

Ukraine conflict: DeepStateMap showed 526-527 frontline polygons all week, a range indicating minimal net territorial change. However, the FIRMS satellite feed consistently showed high-intensity thermal anomalies (14-19 MW FRP, classified as high/nominal confidence) at coordinates around 46.91 N / 34.71 E -- the Zaporizhzhia-Kherson axis -- indicating sustained heavy weapons activity. Wednesday's theater-item count (131 new) was lower than Mon/Tue/Thu, possibly reflecting a midweek operational pause.

Tuesday's headline: Trump announced Russia and Ukraine had agreed to a mutual moratorium on strikes against energy infrastructure. The same day, Ukrainian forces attacked the Syzran refinery. The Kremlin called the idea "very good" but declined to commit to ceasing their own strikes. This is not a truce -- it is a posture. The energy truce announcement functioned primarily as messaging toward Gulf investors and global energy consumers ahead of winter, not as an operational commitment.

A Russian drone was found on a Polish beach near Jaroslaviec on Monday -- the Polish defense minister confirmed the incident. Drone debris was also found near Chisinau (Moldova's capital) overnight Thursday-Friday. Stray Russian drone incidents in Poland and now Moldova within four days of each other: whether these are errors in targeting, navigation failures, or intentional probing of NATO/partnership response thresholds is not determinable from open-source data alone, but the geographic expansion of incidents is notable.

Iran/Hormuz: the week opened with the Iranian commercial vessel strike near Qeshm Island. Chinese business press (Caixin) ran a "Hormuz Weekly" on Monday noting US-Iran military escalation and simultaneous pressure on both the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb. Houthi forces seized the Al-Dhubab Directorate center on Sunday. South Korean civil society protests against deploying Korean military forces to the Hormuz region were large enough to draw Caixin coverage. Meanwhile, shipping companies are accelerating their return to the Red Sea even as Houthi proximity to Bab el-Mandeb grows -- a classic risk-appetite divergence between capital markets and strategic logic.

## Cross-section themes (synthesis layer)

**1. Russia in every feed simultaneously.** This week Russia appeared in: congressional action (Graham Act), OFAC (both additions and removals), DOJ/FARA (RIS Network assassination plot), the federal_register (national emergency continuation), the Ukrainian and Russian internal feeds (energy truce / election), VIP flight data (Belgium/NATO airframes across the eastern European theater), prediction markets (Putin out, United Russia Duma outcome), and the commentary feed (Anthropic's economic paper and the Kremlin deal signals). When a single country saturates this many independent data streams in the same week, it is not noise -- it is a regime under maximum pressure testing in multiple directions simultaneously.

**2. The three-headed US Russia policy.** Congress hardening (Graham Act), OFAC loosening (designation removals), executive dealmaking (Trump-Kremlin business signals). These are not contradictory in a bureaucratic sense -- they represent three different principals operating with different incentive structures. But they are contradictory in their effects on Moscow's calculus and on allied confidence. The Kremlin can thread this gap. Allies cannot calibrate to it.

**3. Energy corridor simultaneity.** In the same week: Iranian vessel struck at Hormuz, Houthis seized Al-Dhubab near Bab el-Mandeb, Trump announced a Ukraine-Russia energy truce that was immediately violated, EPA rescinded greenhouse gas standards for power plants, and oil prices spiked then retreated. These five events each have independent proximate causes but they share a single deep structure: the global energy system is under simultaneous physical, regulatory, and geopolitical stress across multiple chokepoints and policy frameworks. The market interpreted this as volatile but not rupturing. That reading deserves continued scrutiny.

**4. Strasbourg as the new Washington.** Canadian PM Carney addressed the European Parliament, endorsed EU associated membership, and Von der Leyen simultaneously proposed a European Security Council including Canada, Norway, and Ukraine alongside a dedicated hybrid-threat mechanism independent of NATO. This is institutional architecture being built at speed in response to US unpredictability. ECB President Lagarde's speech on "A new age of capital: growth, sovereignty and AI" framed this as the long-term strategic frame: Europe is building autonomous economic and security institutions because it can no longer assume US alignment. The ITAR change removing UUVs from the US Munitions List may also ease transatlantic defense technology transfer -- a small but notable signal in the same direction.

**5. Administrative state transformation at velocity.** The FAR Overhaul (multiple parts simultaneously), EPA GHG rescission, OPM Excepted Service revision, ITAR modifications, and Presidential FY2027 drug transit determination all published this week. The pace is consistent with a pattern visible since January: use regulatory publication velocity to prevent coherent opposition from forming before rules are finalized. Cross-referenced with California's simultaneous legislative burst in the opposite direction, this week is a clear map of where the federal-state fault lines will generate the next decade of litigation.
