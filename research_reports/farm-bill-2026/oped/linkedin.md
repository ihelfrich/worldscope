I read all 460 sections of the 2026 farm bill so you don't have to — and then I couldn't stop thinking about its name.

It's called the **Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026** (H.R. 7567), and it passed the House in April. That third word is doing an enormous amount of work.

The bill reclassifies ordinary agricultural life as a theater of national security: a soybean field, a school lunch, the components inside a rooftop solar panel, a carton of milk, a grove of trees. And "national security" is the most powerful phrase in American government — say it, and environmental review, the courts, and even the appropriations process quietly stand down.

Follow the money and the alarm evaporates:

• Guaranteed farm-loan ceilings doubled to $3.5M — and indexed to land prices
• A new disaster program with a *minimum* payout of $900,000
• The farm-ownership disclosure threshold quietly lifted from 5% to 10%

Meanwhile science, conservation, and food-aid recipients are shown the door. (The SNAP cuts were taken the year before, in the reconciliation bill next door — leaving this one free to wear "Security" in its title.)

I wrote it up as an essay — **"Securitas; or, How to Be Afraid for a Living"** — with the data to back every claim. It's part polemic, part close reading. Link in the comments.

If you read the bill differently, I genuinely want to hear it.
