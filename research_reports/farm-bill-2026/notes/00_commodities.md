# Title I — Commodities — Notes

## Title-level summary
This version of Title I (Sec. 1001–1016) is unusually narrow: it does **not** contain the headline reference-price/PLC/ARC reauthorization or payment-limit increases one normally associates with a Farm Bill commodity title. Instead it concentrates on (a) extending permanent-law suspension and several dairy authorities from 2023 through **2031** (FY2031 reauthorization through-line), (b) a major build-out of disaster assistance — overhauling the **Tree Assistance Program (TAP)**, creating a **new Specialty Crop Emergency Assistance Framework** (new sec. 196A) with payment limits "not less than **$900,000**" for farming-dominant entities, and authorizing **block grants** for uncovered natural-disaster losses, and (c) numerous administrative/processing reforms (loan servicing during shutdowns, electronic disaster forms, mandatory dairy processing-cost reporting). Notable money/policy moves: the $900,000 specialty-crop payment floor; restoration of **tobacco** as a CCC agricultural commodity; expansion of storage-facility loans to **propane** and a study on **fertilizer** storage; and revised evidence standards for **Mexican wolf** livestock depredation. Several provisions carry sunsets (TAP initial-payment authority sunsets **Sept. 30, 2035**). NOTE: This source file for "Title I — Commodities" appears to omit the traditional PLC/ARC reference-price sections; if those exist in the full bill they are not present in this excerpt — no reference prices for covered commodities appear anywhere in the text.

## Section-by-section

### Sec. 1001 — Suspension of permanent price support authority
- **Does:** Extends the suspension of permanent (1938/1949 Act) price-support authority by changing the end year from 2023 to 2031 wherever "2023" appears.
- **Amends:** Sec. 1602 of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (7 U.S.C. 9092).
- **Numbers:** Year change 2023 → 2031.
- **Dates:** Reauthorization through 2031.
- **Type:** EXTEND
- **Affected:** All commodity programs; prevents reversion to permanent-law parity pricing ("dairy cliff"). USDA must administer current programs through 2031.
- **Flag:** Routine but essential — averts the "dairy cliff"/permanent-law reversion.

### Sec. 1002 — Tree assistance program
- **Does:** Overhauls TAP. (a) Broadens definitions — adds "or biennial" after "annual" and "or pest" after "insect" (covers biennial crops and pest losses). (b) Rewrites the economic-viability standard: strikes existing clauses (i)/(ii) and replaces the eligibility trigger so assistance goes to orchardists/nursery growers who "planted trees for commercial purposes but lost the trees or the trees no longer produce an economically viable crop as a result of a natural disaster." (c) Conforms cross-references ("and (5)" → ", (5), (6), and (7)"). (d) Adds new para (6) **Timing requirements** — recipient must complete replacement/rehab within 2 years of approval (or longer if needed for tree survival); new para (7) **Alternatives in replanting** — may replant with alternative variety, stand density, or location, but cost-share is capped at the amount they'd have received replanting the lost variety/density/location. (e) Adds new para (8) **Deadline for notice** — Secretary must approve/deny and notify within 120 days. (f) Adds new para (9) **Initial payments** — grower may opt for an upfront payment equal to fair market value of estimated costs before incurring them, with reconciliation/repayment of overpayments; authority **sunsets Sept. 30, 2035.**
- **Amends:** Sec. 1501(e) of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (7 U.S.C. 9081(e)) — specifically (e)(1), (e)(2)(A), (e)(3), and adds (e)(6)–(e)(9). NOTE: subsection (e) text in (d) and (e) cites both "7 U.S.C. 9081(e)" and an apparent typo "7 U.S.C. 9801(e)"; the correct cite is 9081(e).
- **Numbers:** 2-year completion deadline; 120-day application-decision deadline; initial payment = fair market value of estimated costs; cost-share caps on alternatives.
- **Dates:** Initial-payment authority sunset **September 30, 2035.**
- **Type:** REFORM / NEW / EXPAND
- **Affected:** Orchardists and nursery tree growers (win — faster decisions, upfront cash, replanting flexibility, pest/biennial coverage). USDA/FSA must meet new deadlines.
- **Flag:** 120-day decision deadline and optional upfront payments are grower-friendly; sunset 2035 limits the initial-payment feature.

### Sec. 1003 — Specialty crop emergency assistance framework
- **Does:** Creates a brand-new standing framework (new sec. 196A) directing the Secretary to provide direct assistance to specialty-crop producers hurt by an "adverse event (including an economic crisis or market disruption)." Payment = producer's prior-year (or multi-year average) specialty-crop sales × a Secretary-set payment factor. Secretary must account for the higher value of specialty crops, greater input costs, and diverse legal entity structures. Sets payment limits: generally capped at the Food Security Act sec. 1001(b) limit, BUT for a person/entity whose average gross income is ≥75% from farming/ranching/silviculture, the standard cap does NOT apply and the Secretary sets the max — "except such amount may not be less than **$900,000**." Applies FSA notification/eligibility/denial rules (sec. 1001A(a), 1001B, 1001C). Conforming payment-limit amendments add "or 196A" after "section 196."
- **Amends:** New freestanding provision inserted into the Federal Agriculture Improvement and Reform Act of 1996 after sec. 196 (7 U.S.C. 7333), as new sec. 196A; cross-references Food Security Act of 1985 sec. 1001/1001A/1001B/1001C/1001D (7 U.S.C. 1308 et seq.); amends sec. 1001D(b)(2)(E) and (b)(4)(A)(i)(II) of the Food Security Act of 1985 (7 U.S.C. 1308–3a(b)).
- **Numbers:** Payment factor (Secretary-set); 75% AGI-from-farming threshold; payment floor **not less than $900,000** for farming-dominant entities; standard cap = 7 U.S.C. 1308(b) as adjusted under 1308(i).
- **Dates:** No sunset stated; applies per "crop year."
- **Type:** NEW
- **Affected:** Specialty-crop producers (winners). Large, farming-dominant entities benefit most from the $900,000+ floor. USDA must build the framework.
- **Flag:** Politically salient — a NEW permanent disaster/market-disruption program for specialty crops with a very high ($900,000 floor) payment cap for farming-dominant entities; reporters would lead with this.

### Sec. 1004 — Assistance in the form of block grants
- **Does:** Adds new sec. 1502 allowing the Secretary, when additional funds are made available after enactment for "covered losses," to deliver that assistance as block grants. "Covered losses" = losses of revenue, quality, or production of crops, trees, bushes, vines, poultry or livestock from a natural disaster, for which no other federal assistance is available. Clerical amendment adds sec. 1502 to the table of contents.
- **Amends:** Subtitle E of title I of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (7 U.S.C. 9081 et seq.) — adds new sec. 1502; conforming table-of-contents amendment.
- **Numbers:** None specified (block-grant amounts depend on funds appropriated).
- **Dates:** Applies to funds made available after enactment.
- **Type:** NEW
- **Affected:** States/USDA (block-grant administration); producers with otherwise-uncovered disaster losses.
- **Flag:** Gives USDA discretion to route future ad-hoc disaster money through block grants rather than direct programs.

### Sec. 1005 — Dairy-related extensions
- **Does:** Three dairy extensions. (a) Forward pricing — strikes subsection (e) of the dairy forward-pricing program (removes a sunset/limitation, effectively continuing the program). (b) Dairy Indemnity Program — extends from 2023 to 2031. (c) Dairy promotion and research — extends from 2023 to 2031.
- **Amends:** (a) Sec. 1502 of the Food, Conservation, and Energy Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. 8772); (b) Sec. 3 of Public Law 90–484 (7 U.S.C. 4553); (c) Sec. 113(e)(2) of the Dairy Production Stabilization Act of 1983 (7 U.S.C. 4504(e)(2)).
- **Numbers:** 2023 → 2031.
- **Dates:** Reauthorization through 2031.
- **Type:** EXTEND
- **Affected:** Dairy producers and handlers.
- **Flag:** — (routine extensions).

### Sec. 1006 — Mandatory reporting of dairy product processing costs
- **Does:** Adds a new mandatory reporting requirement: each manufacturer already required to report on a product must also report production cost and product yield information for all products processed in the same facility/facilities. Amends headings from "Electronic reporting" to "Reporting" and narrows certain cross-references to subparagraphs (A) and (B) of subsection (b)(1). Adds new para (3) requiring the Secretary to publish a report on dairy processing costs within 2 years of enactment and every 2 years thereafter. Redesignates old subsection (e) as (f) and adds new subsection (e) exempting Secretary's actions under the section from review under Executive Order 12866 (regulatory review).
- **Amends:** Sec. 273 of the Agricultural Marketing Act of 1946 (7 U.S.C. 1637b).
- **Numbers:** Report within 2 years, then every 2 years.
- **Dates:** First report not later than 2 years after enactment of the paragraph; biennial thereafter.
- **Type:** NEW / REFORM / STUDY-OR-REPORT
- **Affected:** Dairy product manufacturers (new reporting burden); informs Federal Milk Marketing Order make-allowance debates.
- **Flag:** Notable — collects make-allowance/processing-cost data (relevant to FMMO reform) and exempts USDA's actions here from EO 12866 review.

### Sec. 1007 — Dairy reports
- **Does:** Changes the dairy promotion-program reporting cadence/deadline: replaces the existing "Not later than..." annual report language with a per-calendar-year report due not later than 18 months after the last day of each calendar year beginning after enactment of the 2026 Act.
- **Amends:** Para (4) of sec. 301 of the Dairy Production Stabilization Act of 1983 (7 U.S.C. 4514).
- **Numbers:** Report due within 18 months of calendar-year end.
- **Dates:** Applies to each calendar year beginning after enactment of the 2026 Act.
- **Type:** REFORM / STUDY-OR-REPORT
- **Affected:** USDA reporting obligation; dairy checkoff oversight.
- **Flag:** —

### Sec. 1008 — Processing of certain loans
- **Does:** Provides that servicing of (a) marketing assistance loans (sec. 1201) and (b) sugar program loans is "deemed" to be services for emergencies involving the safety of human life or protection of property under 31 U.S.C. 1342 — i.e., USDA can continue servicing these loans during a lapse in appropriations (government shutdown).
- **Amends:** (a) Sec. 1204 of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (7 U.S.C. 9034) — adds new (j); (b) Sec. 156(d) of the Federal Agriculture Improvement and Reform Act of 1996 (7 U.S.C. 7272(d)) — adds new (4).
- **Numbers:** References 31 U.S.C. 1342 (Antideficiency Act exception).
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** NEW / REFORM
- **Affected:** Producers with marketing assistance loans and sugar loans (continuity during shutdowns); USDA staff.
- **Flag:** Notable — uses the Antideficiency Act "safety of human life/property" exception to keep commodity loan servicing running during shutdowns.

### Sec. 1009 — Storage facility loans
- **Does:** Expands the Farm Storage Facility Loan program to authorize loans to producers to construct or upgrade storage facilities for **propane** primarily used for agricultural production (propane defined per 7 CFR 4279.2 as in effect on enactment). Restructures the statute to a list format (producers; and propane-storage producers).
- **Amends:** Sec. 1614(a) of the Food, Conservation, and Energy Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. 8789(a)).
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** NEW / EXPAND
- **Affected:** Producers needing on-farm propane storage (e.g., grain drying, heating).
- **Flag:** Notable expansion of storage-loan eligibility to fuel (propane), not just commodities.

### Sec. 1010 — Strengthening domestic food production supply chains
- **Does:** Adds new sec. 1302 making the "preservation and strengthening" of domestic production of in-subtitle agricultural commodities — specifically commodities from which a food ingredient that serves an important function in the domestic food supply chain is derived — a "priority objective of the President" for any federal policy/rule/guidance affecting those programs. Clerical amendment adds sec. 1302 to the table of contents.
- **Amends:** Subtitle C of title I of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (Public Law 113–79) — adds new sec. 1302; conforming table-of-contents amendment.
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** NEW (policy statement)
- **Affected:** Sugar/other Subtitle C commodity producers; sets a presidential policy priority (commonly read as protective of the domestic sugar industry).
- **Flag:** Notable — a "priority objective of the President" policy directive; widely interpreted as a sugar-industry protection.

### Sec. 1011 — Regulations
- **Does:** Updates rulemaking/administration authority to use a defined "covered provision of law." (a) Replaces a long enumerated list in sec. 1601(c)(2) with "a covered provision of law" and adds a new definition (4) covering: this title + secs. 11003 and 11017; title I of the 2018 Act and its amendments + sec. 10109 of that Act; and title I of the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026 and its amendments. (b) Updates loan-implementation authority (sec. 1614(d)) similarly, defining "covered provision of law" (subtitles B/C and amendments; 2018 Act subtitles B/C amendments except assistance under secs. 1207(c) and 1208; sec. 156 of the 1996 Act/7 U.S.C. 7272) and "repayment provisions."
- **Amends:** Sec. 1601(c) of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (7 U.S.C. 9091(c)); Sec. 1614(d) of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (7 U.S.C. 9097(d)). (References secs. 11003, 11017, 10109, 1207(c), 1208, and sec. 156 of the 1996 Act / 7 U.S.C. 7272.)
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** REFORM (technical/conforming)
- **Affected:** USDA rulemaking authority (extends expedited/specified rulemaking and implementation authority to the 2026 title's provisions).
- **Flag:** Extends streamlined-rulemaking authority to the new 2026 commodity provisions.

### Sec. 1012 — Restoration of tobacco as agricultural commodity in Commodity Credit Corporation Charter Act
- **Does:** Strikes "(other than tobacco)" wherever it appears in the CCC Charter Act, restoring tobacco's status as an agricultural commodity for CCC purposes.
- **Amends:** Sec. 5 of the Commodity Credit Corporation Charter Act (15 U.S.C. 714c).
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** REFORM / restore (effectively reverses a prior exclusion)
- **Affected:** Tobacco — newly eligible for the range of CCC commodity activities.
- **Flag:** Politically salient — restores tobacco to CCC eligibility decades after it was excluded.

### Sec. 1013 — Study on storage facility loans for on-farm fertilizer storage
- **Does:** Directs the Secretary to study the feasibility of providing storage-facility loans for on-farm fertilizer storage, including data on the market (number of farms with such facilities, availability and producer demand), producer access to financing, and a comparison of commercial vs. USDA loan rates/terms. Report due within 1 year of enactment to the House Agriculture Committee and Senate Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry Committee.
- **Amends:** New freestanding provision.
- **Numbers:** None (report 1 year).
- **Dates:** Report not later than 1 year after enactment.
- **Type:** STUDY-OR-REPORT
- **Affected:** USDA (must study); potentially future fertilizer-storage loan eligibility.
- **Flag:** — (pairs with the propane-storage expansion in Sec. 1009).

### Sec. 1014 — Electronic forms for covered disaster assistance programs
- **Does:** Requires the Secretary, as soon as practicable after enactment and in addition to paper forms, to make online electronic enrollment forms available for "covered disaster assistance programs." Defines those as each program under sec. 1501 of the 2014 Act (7 U.S.C. 9081) and the emergency conservation program under title IV of the Agricultural Credit Act of 1978 (16 U.S.C. 2201 et seq.).
- **Amends:** New freestanding provision (references 7 U.S.C. 9081 and 16 U.S.C. 2201 et seq.).
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** As soon as practicable after enactment.
- **Type:** NEW / REFORM (administrative modernization)
- **Affected:** Producers (easier online enrollment); USDA/FSA IT.
- **Flag:** —

### Sec. 1015 — Dairy business innovation initiatives
- **Does:** Increases the number of dairy business innovation initiatives by changing "3" to "4" in two places.
- **Amends:** Sec. 12513 of the Agriculture Improvement Act of 2018 (7 U.S.C. 1632d) — subsections (b) and (g)(1)(A).
- **Numbers:** 3 → 4 (initiatives).
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** INCREASE
- **Affected:** Dairy businesses/regional DBI centers (one additional initiative).
- **Flag:** —

### Sec. 1016 — Revision of evidence standards for livestock indemnity payments for losses by Mexican wolves
- **Does:** Within 180 days of enactment, the Secretary must accept proof of death that does not rely predominantly on subcutaneous hemorrhaging when determining livestock depredation losses caused by Mexican wolves under the Livestock Indemnity Program.
- **Amends:** References para (1)(A) of sec. 1501(b) of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (7 U.S.C. 9081(b)); operates as a freestanding directive.
- **Numbers:** 180 days.
- **Dates:** Within 180 days of enactment.
- **Type:** REFORM
- **Affected:** Ranchers in Mexican wolf range (Southwest) — easier to qualify for indemnity payments.
- **Flag:** Politically salient regionally — eases evidence burden for Mexican-wolf (an endangered species) livestock depredation claims.
