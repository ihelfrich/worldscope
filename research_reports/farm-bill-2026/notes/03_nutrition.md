# Title IV — Nutrition — Notes

## Title-level summary
Title IV reauthorizes SNAP and related nutrition/commodity programs of the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. 2011 et seq.) and allied statutes through FY2031 (the recurring amendment pattern is "striking 2023 and inserting 2031"). The title is organized in three subtitles: A (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, Secs. 4101–4115), B (Commodity Distribution Programs, Secs. 4201–4204), and C (Miscellaneous, Secs. 4301–4310). Substantive SNAP changes are mostly administrative/program-integrity in nature: contractor staffing flexibility tied to a 6% payment error-rate trigger (Sec. 4103), full reporting of all identified payment errors (Sec. 4105), permanent SNAP online purchasing (Sec. 4111), EBT card security rulemaking (Sec. 4108), and several eligible-food expansions (animal protein incentive, hot rotisserie chicken). Subtitles B and C reauthorize and in several cases expand commodity, senior farmers' market, TEFAP, CSFP, Gus Schumacher (GusNIP), dairy nutrition incentives, and healthy-food-financing programs, and create new freestanding programs (Local Farmers Feeding Our Communities, CSFP home-delivery pilot, Tribal self-determination CSFP demonstration). A high-profile non-SNAP reform restructures the Dietary Guidelines process (Sec. 4308). **IMPORTANT — see cross-cutting flags: this engrossed text does NOT contain Thrifty Food Plan cost-neutrality language, ABAWD/work-requirement age expansions, restrictions on broad-based categorical eligibility or immigrant eligibility, state cost-sharing of SNAP benefit dollars, or SNAP-Ed cuts. The error-rate provisions here are administrative (staffing triggers, reporting), not benefit cost-share triggers.**

## Section-by-section

### Sec. 4101 — Declaration of policy
- **Does:** Adds new declaration-of-policy language stating SNAP supports prevention of diet-related chronic disease (obesity, diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, cancer), disability, premature death, unsustainable health care costs, and "undermining of military readiness"; directs the Secretary to administer SNAP to provide participants, "especially children," access to a variety of foods for optimal health. Inserts "(a)" before "It" and adds new subsections (b) and (c).
- **Amends:** Section 2 of the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. 2011).
- **Numbers:** Lists 5 diet-related chronic diseases.
- **Dates:** —
- **Type:** REFORM (declaratory/policy; non-operative on eligibility).
- **Affected:** Sets interpretive policy framing for SNAP administration; no direct beneficiary impact.
- **Flag:** Notable — frames SNAP around chronic-disease prevention and "military readiness," language that supports later food-restriction/healthy-food arguments.

### Sec. 4103 — SNAP staffing flexibility
- **Does:** Adds new subsection (y) allowing a State agency, notwithstanding §11(e)(6)(B), to contract out SNAP certification or any other State-agency function, subject to conditions: contract cannot incentivize delay/denial of eligibility, and contractor must have no direct/indirect financial interest in an approved retail store. Authorizes use when: (A) State cannot timely process applications (pandemics/health emergencies, seasonal workforce cycles, temporary staffing shortages, weather/natural disasters); (B) the State's payment error rate (per §16) is **≥ 6 percent** on most recent USDA data; or (C) the State sees an increase in applications. Requires consistency with 5 CFR 900.603 principles and a "blended workforce" that does not supplant merit-based personnel. Requires State notification to the Secretary; Secretary must post notification publicly within 10 days. Such contracting is not a "major change" under §11(a)(4). Requires an annual report to House/Senate Ag committees. For temporary staffing shortages, authority expires when the application backlog is eliminated, cannot override collective bargaining agreements/MOUs, and expires when the error rate (per §16) is **< 6 percent**.
- **Amends:** Section 11 (7 U.S.C. 2020), adding subsection (y).
- **Numbers:** Payment error-rate trigger **6 percent** (≥6% to use authority; expires for temp shortages when <6%); public posting within **10 days**.
- **Dates:** Annual report; no sunset stated.
- **Type:** REFORM / NEW authority.
- **Affected:** States (gain contracting flexibility, esp. high-error-rate states); public/private contractors; state merit employees (protected by anti-supplant/CBA clauses); applicants (anti-delay/anti-denial safeguards).
- **Flag:** Notable — ties contractor authority to a 6% payment error-rate threshold. NOTE: this is an administrative error-rate trigger for staffing, **not** a state benefit cost-share trigger.

### Sec. 4308 — Dietary guidelines
- **Does:** Restructures the Dietary Guidelines for Americans process. Changes frequency from "At least every five years" to "Beginning with the 2030 report and at least every 10 years thereafter," and applies APA §553 rulemaking to each report. Rewrites the evidentiary standard: guidelines must be based on "significant scientific agreement" via "evidence-based review" (defined), be current, derive from questions generated by an Independent Advisory Board, address high-priority concerns, achieve nutritional adequacy, include info for chronic-disease populations, and be "affordable, available, and accessible." Adds: more-frequent publication option; 90-day advance notification to Senate Ag/HELP and House Ag/Energy & Commerce committees with justification; establishment of an Independent Advisory Board (4–8 members; 4 appointed by Secretaries, 2 of whom non-federal; up to 1 per relevant committee by ranking minority-party member; nutrition/food-science expertise) that submits scientific questions within 1 year then disbands. Adds an "Exclusion" barring guidance on topics not relevant to dietary guidance (taxation, social welfare policy, Federal feeding-program purchases, food/ag production practices, food labeling, socioeconomic status, race, religion, ethnicity, culture, nutrition regulations). Adds "evidence-based review" definition (totality of evidence, standardized rating methods, external nongovernment peer review), strength-of-evidence ratings (incl. Healthy Eating Index impact), and transparency requirements (advisory members as special government employees, OGE Form 450 financial disclosure, 10-year funding/affiliation reporting, public disclosure within 30 days, conflict-of-interest management plan). (b) The 2025 Dietary Guidelines remain controlling until the first report under the amended section.
- **Amends:** Section 301(a) of the National Nutrition Monitoring and Related Research Act of 1990 (7 U.S.C. 5341(a)).
- **Numbers:** Frequency every **10 years** (from 5); Board **4–8** members; 90-day notice; 30-day disclosure publication; 10-year affiliation lookback.
- **Dates:** "Beginning with the 2030 report"; 2025 DGA controlling until first new report.
- **Type:** REFORM (major restructuring) + STUDY/PROCESS.
- **Affected:** USDA/HHS, Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee, nutrition-science community, Congress; downstream effects on all federal feeding programs that key off the DGA.
- **Flag:** Notable/controversial — moves DGA to a 10-year cycle, adds APA rulemaking, an Independent Advisory Board, and a broad "exclusion" list (bars consideration of socioeconomic status, race, culture, ag production practices, food labeling, taxation, etc.) in the guidelines.

## Cross-cutting flags for this title
- **Reauthorization horizon:** Core SNAP and most allied programs extended from FY2023 to **FY2031** (recurring "2023"→"2031" amendments). Several new/expanded programs funded **FY2027–FY2031**.
- **Error-rate provisions are administrative, NOT a benefit cost-share:** The only payment-error-rate thresholds in this title are in **Sec. 4103** (6% trigger for contractor staffing flexibility; expires <6% for temporary shortages) and **Sec. 4105** (report all identified errors, regardless of dollar amount, without changing the small-error tolerance). **There is NO provision in this engrossed Title IV requiring states to share in SNAP benefit costs based on payment error rate, and no increase in the state share of administrative costs tied to error rates.**
- **ABSENT — Thrifty Food Plan:** No section in this title re-evaluates or constrains the Thrifty Food Plan, and no cost-neutrality requirement on TFP re-evaluation appears. (None of Secs. 4101–4115 touch §3(u)/TFP.)
- **ABSENT — ABAWD / work requirements:** No expansion of work requirements, no change to ABAWD age ranges, time limits, or exemptions appears anywhere in this title.
- **ABSENT — Eligibility restrictions:** No provisions restricting SNAP eligibility by immigration status, and no change to broad-based categorical eligibility, appear in this title.
- **ABSENT — SNAP-Ed and other cuts:** No SNAP-Ed (nutrition education) cut or repeal appears; the title contains no DECREASE/REPEAL of benefit levels or program funding. Direction of substantive changes is generally neutral-to-expansionary (new local-food purchasing, dairy incentive increase $20M→$50M, HFFI $125M→$135M, new pilots).
- **Food-choice / "what SNAP can buy" trajectory:** Sec. 4101 (chronic-disease framing), Sec. 4110 (animal protein incentive), Sec. 4114 (hot rotisserie chicken eligible), and Sec. 4115 (report on demonstration projects redefining eligible "food," with legislative recommendations) collectively set up future debate over restricting/expanding eligible SNAP foods — but impose no restriction here.
- **Buy American / geopolitical:** Sec. 4302 imposes a hard 95% domestic-purchase floor for school meals with narrow waivers and a China/Russia poultry & seafood import ban — the most restriction-heavy provision in the title (school meals, not SNAP).
- **Dietary Guidelines overhaul (Sec. 4308):** Most consequential non-SNAP reform — 10-year cycle, APA rulemaking, Independent Advisory Board, conflict-of-interest transparency, and a broad "exclusion list" barring consideration of socioeconomic status, race, culture, taxation, ag production practices, and food labeling. Affects all programs keyed to the DGA. 2025 DGA controls in the interim.
- **New spending programs:** Local Farmers Feeding Our Communities ($200M/yr FY2027–2031, ~$1B); CSFP home-delivery pilot ($10M/yr); Tribal CSFP self-determination demo ($1M + $1.2M/yr staffing); dairy incentive increase; HFFI increase.
- **State compliance levers:** Sec. 4309 ("may have funds withheld" for child-care food-safety noncompliance) is the only fund-withholding mechanism; Sec. 4103 anti-supplant/CBA protections constrain how states use contractor flexibility.
- **Transcription note:** Sec. 4106 source shows `"2031` (unbalanced quote) — an OCR/formatting artifact; substance is reauthorization through FY2031.
