# Title III — Trade — Notes

## Title-level summary
Title III reauthorizes U.S. agricultural trade and international food aid programs through FY2031 and makes one structural change of major significance: it transfers administration of the Food for Peace Act (Public Law 480 / Title II emergency and development food aid) from the USAID Administrator to the Secretary of Agriculture, with assets, liabilities, contracts, and rulemaking authority all moving to USDA. It substantially boosts and restructures trade-promotion funding under the Agricultural Trade Act of 1978, raising mandatory CCC funding to $500M (FY2027) and $533M (FY2028–2031), with specified floors for the Market Access Program (MAP) and Foreign Market Development (FMD) cooperator program. It contains a high-profile "common names" provision protecting U.S. use of generic food/wine/beer terms (parmesan, feta, etc.) in foreign markets, new national-security/enforcement task forces (including a directive aimed at India's minimum price supports), and Sense-of-Congress language opposing expanded Argentine beef imports. It reauthorizes Food for Progress, McGovern-Dole, the Bill Emerson Humanitarian Trust, the Farmer-to-Farmer program, the Global Crop Diversity Trust, and other programs through FY2031. New mandatory set-asides include $200M/year for ready-to-use therapeutic foods to address child wasting and $15M/year for the International Food Relief Partnership.

## Section-by-section

### Sec. 3101 — Transfer of authorities to the Secretary of Agriculture
- **Does:** Transfers Food for Peace Act administration from the USAID Administrator to the Secretary of Agriculture. Strikes "(to be implemented by the Administrator)" and inserts "(to be implemented by the Secretary)" in Sec. 201. Conforming amendments replace "Administrator" with "Secretary" throughout Secs. 202, 203, 205, 207, 208 and Title III. Strikes the "Administrator" definition (old para (1)) in Sec. 402 and redesignates paras (2)–(9) as (1)–(8). Strikes "or the Administrator, as appropriate" language in Secs. 403–404. Repeals Sec. 405 (Consultation). Adds new Title VII (Secs. 701–704): transfers USAID assets/liabilities/contracts/grants/loans/licenses (pursuant to authority on/after Jan 1, 2026) to USDA; transfers other statutory authorities; directs the Secretary to promulgate/amend rules (including interim final rules) to effectuate the transfer; requires the Secretary to consult with the Secretary of State.
- **Amends:** Food for Peace Act, Secs. 201, 202, 203, 205, 207, 208 (7 U.S.C. 1721, 1722, 1723, 1725, 1726a, 1726b); Title III (7 U.S.C. 1727 et seq.); Secs. 402, 403, 404, 405 (7 U.S.C. 1732, 1733, 1734, 1735); adds new Title VII to 7 U.S.C. 1691 et seq.
- **Numbers:** —
- **Dates:** Asset transfer applies to authority exercised on/after January 1, 2026; effective on enactment.
- **Type:** REFORM (major structural reorganization)
- **Affected:** USDA gains administration of food aid (winner); USAID loses it (loser); NGOs/implementers must deal with USDA. State Dept retained in a consultative role only.
- **Flag:** Notable/controversial — moves Food for Peace out of USAID entirely; consolidates international food aid under USDA, a long-debated reorganization.

### Sec. 3201 — Agricultural trade promotion and facilitation
- **Does:** (a) Adds new para (4) to FMD cooperator program (Sec. 203(c)): Secretary shall contract with eligible trade orgs / supply-chain nonprofits for needs assessments, training, and technical assistance to improve foreign-market infrastructure (cold chain, ports, etc.) protecting U.S. commodities — capped at not more than $1,500,000 (FY2027) and not more than $5,000,000 (FY2028 and each FY thereafter). (b) Rewrites Sec. 203(e)(7) into a detailed biennial report (Secretary + USTR) on U.S. specialty-crop competitiveness covering foreign tariffs/quotas, nontariff/SPS barriers, food-safety differences, Sec. 301 actions, WTO actions, public/advisory-committee comment, machine-readable form; defines appropriate committees (House Ag & Ways and Means; Senate Ag, Nutrition, & Forestry and Finance). (c) Restructures and increases Sec. 203(f) mandatory CCC funding: FY2026 $255,000,000; FY2027 $500,000,000; FY2028–2031 $533,000,000/year (to remain available until expended). Sets program floors — MAP (clause (i)): $200M (FY2026), $400M (FY2027), $410M (FY2028–2031); FMD (clause (ii)): $34.5M (FY2026), $70.5M (FY2027), $82M (FY2028–2031); clause (iii) emerging markets not more than $8M (FY2026 & 2027), $16M (FY2028–2031); clause (iv) (TASC/CCC) $9M (FY2026), $18M (FY2027–2031); clause (v)(I) specialty crops $3.5M (FY2026 & 2027), $7M (FY2028–2031). - **Dates:** FY2026 through FY2031.
- **Type:** INCREASE / REFORM / STUDY-OR-REPORT / REPEAL

### Sec. 3202 — Preserving foreign markets for goods using common names
- **Does:** (a) Amends Sec. 102 definitions of the Ag Trade Act of 1978: reorganizes/redesignates paragraphs and inserts a NEW definition of "common name" — a name ordinarily/customarily used for an ag commodity or food product, typically on packaging/label, including wine varietal/traditional terms (but excluding listed appellations of origin), consistent with Codex standards. Provides an extensive ENUMERATED list of common names for foods (e.g., asiago, brie, bologna, cheddar, chevre, feta, fontina, gorgonzola, gouda, gruyere, havarti, munster, parmesan, pecorino, prosciutto, provolone, ricotta, romano, swiss, etc.), wine (grape varietal terms, TTB-approved designations, nonvarietal descriptors like chateau/tawny/vintage), and beer (pale ale, IPA, porter, stout, pilsener, lager, etc.). Lets the Secretary consider dictionaries, standards (incl. Codex), and customary use; rule of construction lets Secretary recognize additional common names. Adds new clause (vii) treating prohibition/disallowance of a recognized common name as a covered "barrier." (b) Adds new Sec. 303 to Title III: Secretary coordinates with USTR to secure U.S. producers'/exporters' right to use common names in foreign markets via bilateral/plurilateral/multilateral agreements, MOUs, or letter exchanges; requires twice-annual joint briefing to House Ag & Ways and Means and Senate Ag and Finance committees.
- **Amends:** Agricultural Trade Act of 1978 Sec. 102 (7 U.S.C. 5602); adds Sec. 303 to Title III (7 U.S.C. 5652 et seq.).
- **Numbers:** —
- **Dates:** Twice-annual briefings (no sunset stated).
- **Type:** NEW / REFORM
- **Affected:** U.S. cheese, meat, wine, and beer producers/exporters (winners); directly targets EU geographical-indications (GI) restrictions (foreign GI holders are the implicit losers).
- **Flag:** Notable/controversial — codifies the "common names" fight against EU GIs; statutorily lists generic terms the U.S. will defend.

## Cross-cutting flags for this title
- **Major institutional shift:** Food for Peace administration moves wholesale from USAID to USDA (Secs. 3101–3113), including asset/liability/contract transfer, new rulemaking authority, and FAS-funded administration. State Dept relegated to consultation. This recurs as the "Administrator → Secretary" swap across nearly every Subtitle A and several Subtitle C sections.
- **Trade-promotion funding sharply increased and made permanent-floor:** Sec. 3201 raises total Ag Trade Act funding to $533M/year by FY2028 (from $255M baseline) with hard floors — MAP up to $410M, FMD up to $82M — the most significant MAP/FMD increase in decades, plus new foreign-market infrastructure technical assistance.
- **National-security / foreign-adversary and enforcement posture:** Sec. 3311 directs a WTO challenge to India's minimum price supports; Sec. 3203 targets seasonal-produce import surges; Sec. 3401 (USMCA) and Sec. 3402 (Argentine beef) reflect defensive trade posture. Note: no explicit foreign-adversary farmland/ownership provision appears in this title (those typically sit elsewhere in the bill).
- **Common names / EU-GI fight codified:** Sec. 3202 enumerates generic food, wine, and beer names the U.S. will defend and mandates USTR/USDA negotiations and briefings — a direct counter to EU geographical-indications restrictions.
- **Consistent reauthorization horizon:** Nearly every program extended from 2023 to 2031 (FY2031), matching the bill's FY2031 reauthorization frame.
- **New mandatory set-asides:** $200M/year RUTFs for child wasting (conditional triggers; Sec. 3110); $15M/year International Food Relief Partnership (Sec. 3106); $10M/year youth ag exchange program (Sec. 3306).
- **Earmark-style favoritism:** Sec. 3306 priority clause favors a nonprofit holding a pre-2019 MOU; Sec. 3201 directs infrastructure TA to specific nonprofit types.
- **Reorientation away from "humanitarian" framing:** Food for Progress (Sec. 3302) strips "humanitarian" language in favor of "development."
- **Reporting burden:** Multiple new mandated reports — specialty-crop competitiveness biennial report, shrimp trade GAO report, USMCA impact report, Argentine beef report, quarterly enforcement task force reports, women-and-girls food-aid assessment.
