# Title IX — Energy — Notes

## Title-level summary
Title IX (Energy) of H.R. 7567 EH reauthorizes the USDA energy programs created under Title IX of the Farm Security and Rural Investment Act of 2002 (FSRIA, 7 U.S.C. 8101–8115), extending sunset/funding dates from 2023/2024/2026 to 2031 across most surviving programs. The four flagship programs are treated unevenly: Biorefinery Assistance (Sec. 9003) and the Bioenergy Program for Advanced Biofuels (Sec. 9005) keep their existing mandatory funding ("such sums" / continued CCC funding) but the source text does NOT restate a specific mandatory dollar amount for either — it only extends the funding-authorization year to 2031 (and Sec. 9003 raises a per-project loan-guarantee cap and rescinds $18M). REAP/Rural Energy for America Program (Sec. 9007) is extended to 2031, restructured with a new reserve fund, streamlined applications, and a higher grant cap, but again the source text shows only the year change (striking 2023, inserting 2031), not a restated annual mandatory figure. The BioPreferred/Biobased Markets program (Sec. 9002) is extended to 2031 and given new procurement-accountability and NAICS/NAPCS-code reporting duties. The title repeals three programs (Biodiesel Fuel Education, Carbon Utilization and Biogas Education, and effectively replaces program slots), and adds substantial NEW freestanding provisions on solar-on-farmland (a study plus a hard funding limitation with foreign-entity-of-concern sourcing bans), sustainable aviation fuels, pumping-system efficiency, second-use batteries, and a tree-planting grant program. Net thrust: extend-and-reform the bioeconomy programs while erecting new guardrails against converting farmland to ground-mounted solar.

## Section-by-section

### Sec. 9001 — Definition of advanced biofuel
- **Does:** Adds "and sustainable aviation fuel" after "diesel-equivalent fuel" in the definition of "advanced biofuel," expanding what counts as advanced biofuel to include SAF.
- **Amends:** Sec. 9001(3)(B)(iv) of FSRIA 2002 (7 U.S.C. 8101(3)(B)(iv)).
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** REFORM (definitional expansion).
- **Affected:** SAF producers (winners — newly eligible across Title IX programs keyed to the advanced-biofuel definition).
- **Flag:** Threads SAF through the whole title's eligibility; pairs with Sec. 9013 SAF strategy.

### Sec. 9002 — Biobased markets program (BioPreferred)
- **Does:** (1) Adds new subsec. (a)(2)(G): OFPP, with the Secretary, must give procuring agencies educational materials to weigh product longevity, economic savings, efficacy and performance. (2) Overhauls reporting in (a)(4): strikes old clause (ii) and redesignates; converts agency reporting into "a report that describes, for the year covered" including actions taken to establish/implement biobased procurement programs; adds new items (VI) listing biobased-product categories unavailable to meet procurement needs plus desired performance characteristics, and (VII) explanation of procurement requirements agencies failed to meet and reasons. (3) Adds new (a)(4)(D) "Accountability": OFPP, in consultation with Secretary, must annually collect and publicly post the reported information, document which agencies have established procurement programs, and make it public subject to FOIA exemptions (5 U.S.C. 552(b)). (4) In subsec. (f): adds "and NAPCS" codes / North American Product Classification System codes alongside NAICS; replaces (f)(1)(A)/(B) with "renewable chemicals manufacturers and biobased products manufacturers" and "renewable chemicals and biobased products"; adds new (f)(2) report (due within 90 days of enactment of the paragraph) to House/Senate Ag committees on bioeconomy data in NAICS/NAPCS codes, recommendations for the 2027 NAICS/NAPCS revisions, and impact assessment.
- **Amends:** Sec. 9002 of FSRIA 2002 (7 U.S.C. 8102).
- **Numbers:** No dollar authorization changes shown.
- **Dates:** Subsec. (k)(1): strike "2024" → insert "2031"; subsec. (k)(2): strike "2023" → insert "2031" (reauthorization to FY2031). New (f)(2) report due 90 days after enactment; 2027 NAICS/NAPCS revision cycle referenced.
- **Type:** EXTEND + REFORM + STUDY-OR-REPORT.
- **Affected:** OFPP and federal procuring agencies (must report and act); biobased/renewable-chemical manufacturers (winners — better data classification and procurement visibility).
- **Flag:** New public-accountability/naming-and-shaming mechanism for agencies that fail biobased procurement requirements.

### Sec. 9003 — Biorefinery assistance
- **Does:** (1) In (b)(1): inserts "or innovative" before "commercial-scale" and adds ", renewable chemicals, or biobased products" after "end-user products" — broadening eligible projects. (2) In (d)(1): restructures the feasibility paragraph (B) and adds a WAIVER letting the Secretary waive the commercial-viability demonstration for projects adopting commercially available technology; redesignates (C)/(D) as (D)/(E); inserts new (C) "Technical review agreement" requiring the Secretary to enter an agreement with each applicant defining objectives/outcomes/conditions for technical feasibility, with detailed conditions (integrated demonstration unit timeline, evaluation criteria/third-party assessments, feedstock-yield criteria, operation duration/quality/quantity specs), a noncompliance-notice process, a corrective-action period of not more than 90 days, and a technical-feasibility approval step. (3) In (d)(2)(A): strikes "$250,000,000" and inserts "$400,000,000" — raising the maximum loan-guarantee amount per project (or aggregate cap) from $250M to $400M. (4) In subsec. (g) Funding: restructures to "(g) Funding. There ... is authorized" and strikes "2023" → "2031." (5) RESCISSION: rescinds $18,000,000 of unobligated balances under section 9003.
- **Amends:** Sec. 9003 of FSRIA 2002 (7 U.S.C. 8103).
- **Numbers:** Loan-guarantee cap $250,000,000 → $400,000,000 (INCREASE). Rescission of $18,000,000 unobligated balances (DECREASE). NOTE: the source text does not restate the program's underlying mandatory CCC funding amount; only the funding-year is changed to 2031.
- **Dates:** Funding reauthorized through 2031; 90-day corrective-action period.
- **Type:** EXTEND + REFORM + INCREASE (per-project cap) + partial RESCISSION.
- **Affected:** Biorefinery/renewable-chemical/biobased-product project developers (winners — bigger guarantees, waiver, innovative projects newly eligible); applicants face new technical-review-agreement compliance burden.
- **Flag:** $18M rescission of unobligated balances is the only hard cut; per-project cap jump to $400M is notable.

### Sec. 9004 — Bioproduct labeling terminology
- **Does:** Creates a NEW freestanding section 9004 (inserted after sec. 9003 of FSRIA). Within 1 year of enactment the Secretary must issue rules setting national uniform labeling standards for proper use of: "bio-attributed plastic," "bio-attributed product," "biobased plastic," and "plant-based product." Must include the section-9001-defined terms biobased product, intermediate ingredient or feedstock, renewable biomass, and renewable chemical. Requires consultation with biomanufacturers, R&D entities, feedstock growers, and other industry stakeholders.
- **Amends:** Title IX of FSRIA 2002 (7 U.S.C. 8101–8115) — NEW freestanding provision (new sec. 9004).
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** Rules due within 1 year of enactment.
- **Type:** NEW.
- **Affected:** Biomanufacturers, plastics/bioproduct marketers (new labeling rules); USDA must rulemake.
- **Flag:** Anti-greenwashing labeling standardization for "plant-based"/"biobased plastic" claims.

### Sec. 9005 — Bioenergy program for advanced biofuels
- **Does:** Extends the program's funding authorization year only.
- **Amends:** Sec. 9005(g)(2) of FSRIA 2002 (7 U.S.C. 8105(g)(2)) — strike "2023" → insert "2031."
- **Numbers:** No dollar figure restated in source; underlying mandatory CCC funding continues by virtue of the date extension (amount not shown).
- **Dates:** Reauthorized through FY2031.
- **Type:** EXTEND.
- **Affected:** Advanced-biofuel producers (winners — continued payments).
- **Flag:** — (one-line date extension; mandatory funding preserved by extending the funding year rather than converting to discretionary).

### Sec. 9006 — Biodiesel Fuel Education Program
- **Does:** Repeals the program entirely.
- **Amends:** Sec. 9006 of FSRIA 2002 (7 U.S.C. 8106) — REPEALED.
- **Numbers:** Eliminates program funding (prior law provided mandatory funding for biodiesel education; amount not restated here).
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** REPEAL.
- **Affected:** Biodiesel education grantees/cooperators (losers).
- **Flag:** One of three outright repeals in this title.

### Sec. 9007 — Rural Energy for America Program (REAP)
- **Does:** Major reform package. (1) Adds shorthand "(referred to in this section as the Program)." (2) In (b)(3) feasibility-study factors: inserts ", cost savings," after "savings" and adds new (G) — potential to "meaningfully improve the financial conditions" of the producer/rural small business. (3) In (c): adds "agricultural cooperatives with less than 2,500 employees" as eligible for loan guarantees/grants under (c)(1)(A)(i); adds new (c)(2)(G) financial-condition-improvement factor; and in (c)(3)(B) strikes "$25,000,000" → inserts "$50,000,000" (doubling that cap). (4) Redesignates (d)/(e)/(f) → (e)/(f)/(g) and inserts NEW (d) "Streamlined application process" (including within each tier, with bundled applications for combined grant+financial-assistance projects). (5) In renumbered (e): retitles "Outreach" → "Outreach, technical assistance, and education," restructures to add (2) technical assistance for applicants and (3) outreach/technical assistance/education to recipients on integrating renewable energy on land shared with crops or livestock (agrivoltaics). (6) In renumbered (g): strike "2023" → "2031" (funding through FY2031); adds NEW (g)(4) "Reserve fund" — not less than 10% of funds obligated each fiscal year deposited in a Treasury reserve fund for grants supporting "underutilized renewable energy technologies," grant capped at 25% of installation/maintenance costs, at least 2 solicitations per fiscal year, unobligated reserve funds reallocated to the program after 1 year; "underutilized" defined as technologies receiving ≤20% of the average total amounts made available under the section over the 5 most recent fiscal years. (7) Adds NEW (h) "Project diversity" — Secretary must ensure diversity of technologies/products/approaches in approvals. (b) Conforming: strike "subsection (f)" → "subsection (g)" each place.
- **Amends:** Sec. 9007 of FSRIA 2002 (7 U.S.C. 8107).
- **Numbers:** Grant cap (c)(3)(B): $25,000,000 → $50,000,000 (INCREASE). New reserve-fund set-aside: ≥10% of obligated funds; reserve grants ≤25% of project install/maintenance cost; "underutilized" threshold = ≤20% of 5-year average. NOTE: source text shows only the funding-year change ("2023"→"2031"); it does NOT restate REAP's underlying mandatory annual funding amount.
- **Dates:** Reauthorized/funded through FY2031; ≥2 solicitations per fiscal year; reserve reallocation 1 year after fiscal year.
- **Type:** EXTEND + REFORM + INCREASE.
- **Affected:** Agricultural producers, rural small businesses, now agricultural cooperatives (<2,500 employees) — winners. New reserve fund favors underutilized/emerging renewable technologies. Agrivoltaics (shared crop/livestock + renewable) explicitly supported.
- **Flag:** Mandatory funding preserved via date extension (not converted to discretionary); doubled grant cap and new 10% emerging-tech reserve are the headline reforms.

### Sec. 9011 — Study on effects of solar panel installations on covered farmland
- **Does:** Creates NEW freestanding sec. 9015. Secretary (with Secretary of Energy) must study effects of solar installations on conversion of covered farmland out of ag production: economic effects (crop yields, land values incl. adjacent, land access/tenure, local economies, food security); soil/water/wildlife/vegetation/drainage/air-quality impacts of installation, operation, decommissioning + best practices; shared solar+ag (agrivoltaics) best practices incl. reducing nonfarmer ownership and enhancing biodiversity; land best/worst suited; livestock-panel compatibility (height, spacing, manure, fencing); crop-panel compatibility (height, spacing, equipment, yield); degree to which existing tax incentives drive farmland development; incentives to shift solar to built environment/brownfields/contaminated sites; effectiveness of federal programs; and estimate of agricultural-production loss. Consult Energy plus farmers, ranchers, landowners, ag organizations, State ag/energy departments, local governments, conservation organizations, land-grant colleges, solar developers.
- **Amends:** Title IX of FSRIA 2002 — NEW freestanding provision (new sec. 9015).
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** Report due within 2 years of enactment to House Ag + Energy & Commerce and Senate Ag + Energy & Natural Resources committees.
- **Type:** STUDY-OR-REPORT (NEW).
- **Affected:** USDA/DOE; solar developers and farmland owners (subject of study).
- **Flag:** Defines "covered farmland" (Farmland Protection Policy Act 7 U.S.C. 4201(c)(1) + nonindustrial private forest land 16 U.S.C. 3801(a)(18)); pairs directly with the hard limitation in Sec. 9012.

### Sec. 9012 — Limitation on USDA funding for ground-mounted solar energy systems
- **Does:** Creates NEW freestanding sec. 9016. (b) Secretary MAY NOT provide financial assistance for a project that would result in conversion of covered farmland for solar energy production. (c) Exception if the project converts <5 acres, OR converts <50 acres with the majority of energy for on-farm use AND a resolution of approval/support from each county and municipality where sited. (d) For (c)(2) projects: applicant must develop a farmland conservation plan (best practices to protect soil health, mitigate erosion/compaction during construction/operation/decommissioning; remediate/restore soil health) and ensure sufficient decommissioning/remediation funds; Secretary may obligate but not disburse until compliance verified; plan must be carried out during production and on cessation; noncompliance requires repayment of the full amount of financial assistance. (e) Additional limitation: no financial assistance for a project procuring a solar energy component (defined 26 U.S.C. 45x(c)(3)) produced/manufactured/assembled in a foreign country of concern (CHIPS Act 2022 sec. 10638(2), 42 U.S.C. 19237(2)) or by an entity domiciled/controlled by such a country or a foreign entity of concern (sec. 10638(3), 42 U.S.C. 19237(3)).
- **Amends:** Title IX of FSRIA 2002 — NEW freestanding provision (new sec. 9016).
- **Numbers:** Acreage thresholds: <5 acres; <50 acres (with conditions); decommissioning-fund and full-repayment requirements.
- **Dates:** None specific.
- **Type:** NEW (REFORM/restriction on existing programs incl. REAP funding).
- **Affected:** Solar developers and farmers seeking USDA solar funding (losers — major new restriction); domestic solar-component makers (relative winners); Chinese/foreign-entity-of-concern solar supply chains (excluded).
- **Flag:** Hard funding prohibition + foreign-entity-of-concern sourcing ban; this is the most consequential policy shift in the title. Effectively limits how REAP and other USDA programs can fund ground-mounted solar on farmland.

### Sec. 9013 — Sustainable aviation fuels strategy
- **Does:** Secretary must establish a Department-wide strategy to advance SAF production by: facilitating cross-mission-area collaboration on the SAF supply chain (incl. crops grown for SAF); identifying opportunities to maximize SAF development/deployment/commercialization; leveraging farmers/ranchers/foresters/producers; supporting rural economic development via SAF; promoting public-private partnerships.
- **Amends:** Freestanding provision (not codified into FSRIA; directive to the Secretary).
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** None specified.
- **Type:** NEW (strategy/directive).
- **Affected:** USDA mission areas; SAF feedstock growers and producers (winners).
- **Flag:** Complements Sec. 9001 SAF definitional inclusion.

### Sec. 9014 — Leveraging efficiency awareness for pumping systems
- **Does:** Findings on irrigation/pumping energy (600,000+ irrigation pumping systems; potential 22 billion kWh/yr and 8.3 million metric tons CO2 savings; >$1.8 billion/yr savings; $2.3 billion aquaculture industry; etc.). (b) Within 180 days of enactment, Secretary (consulting pumping experts) must publish accessible info on cost/energy/water/carbon savings from efficient pumping systems, including pumps/pipes/motors/drives/controls and USDA programs (EQIP, REAP, CSP) that fund them. (c) Within 180 days, develop a public energy-efficiency preassessment tool (user inputs pump type, flow, pressure, speed ratings/actuals; outputs projected savings). (d) Within 180 days, establish a process to educate USDA energy auditors on pumping-system efficiency. (e) Amends Sec. 1240I(2)(B)(i) of the Food Security Act of 1985 (16 U.S.C. 3839aa–21(2)(B)(i)) to insert "and energy-efficient pumping systems." (f) Defines "pumping system."
- **Amends:** Mostly freestanding directives; (e) amends 16 U.S.C. 3839aa–21 (Conservation Stewardship Program).
- **Numbers:** Findings figures only (not authorizations).
- **Dates:** Three 180-day deadlines after enactment.
- **Type:** NEW (education/tools) + REFORM (CSP eligibility).
- **Affected:** Farmers/ranchers/aquaculture operators (winners — new tools and CSP coverage); USDA must build tools and train auditors.
- **Flag:** Adds energy-efficient pumping systems to CSP activities — small but concrete program change buried in an awareness section.

### Sec. 9015 — Adding waste energy recovery to the Rural Energy for America Program
- **Does:** Expands the definition of "renewable energy" to include "waste energy recovery."
- **Amends:** Sec. 9001(15)(A) of FSRIA 2002 (7 U.S.C. 8101(15)(A)) — strike "or hydroelectric" and insert "hydroelectric, or waste energy recovery."
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** None.
- **Type:** REFORM (definitional expansion).
- **Affected:** Waste-energy-recovery projects (winners — now REAP-eligible renewable energy).
- **Flag:** Title-numbering quirk — this is "Sec. 9015" of the bill but amends the section-9001 definition; do not confuse with the new FSRIA sec. 9015 created by bill Sec. 9011.

### Sec. 9016 — Study on utilization of second-use batteries for agricultural purposes
- **Does:** Creates NEW freestanding sec. 9017 (FSRIA). Secretary (with Secretary of Energy) studies feasibility/costs/benefits/barriers to deploying second-use EV batteries on farms/ranches: potential to support ag during power outages; availability/projected supply of retired EV batteries; cost-effectiveness vs. new storage; safety standards and liability; adequacy of federal programs; recommendations for federal actions incl. potential grant or cost-share programs.
- **Amends:** Title IX of FSRIA 2002 — NEW freestanding provision (new sec. 9017).
- **Numbers:** None.
- **Dates:** Report due within 1 year of enactment to House Ag + Energy & Commerce and Senate Ag + Energy & Natural Resources committees.
- **Type:** STUDY-OR-REPORT (NEW).
- **Affected:** USDA/DOE; agricultural producers (potential future beneficiaries).
- **Flag:** Defines "electric drive vehicle" (sec. 641(b)(3) of the U.S. Energy Storage Competitiveness Act of 2007) and "second-use battery." Note: subsections jump (a),(b),(d),(e) — no (c) in the engrossed text.

### Sec. 9017 — Tree planting grant program
- **Does:** Creates a NEW freestanding grant program (not codified into FSRIA). Within 90 days of enactment, Secretary establishes a program to award grants to eligible entities for "covered projects" (tree planting to reduce residential energy consumption), consulting Secretary of Energy. Applications must describe energy-consumption reduction, estimated reduction, eligible costs/other funding, community engagement, and tree species/suitability. Priority to projects: largest energy reduction for high-energy-burden households; max shade/wind protection; low-canopy neighborhoods; high senior/children populations; below-regional-median income areas; community engagement; local (esp. unemployed/underemployed) workforce. Tree-planting goal: ≥300,000 trees each year (subject to appropriations). Federal share 90%. Defines covered project, eligible cost (planning/design, nurseries, purchasing/site prep/planting trees; maintenance/monitoring up to 3 years; training; other), eligible entity (State/local gov, Indian Tribe, nonprofit 501(c)(3), retail power provider), energy burden, etc.
- **Amends:** Freestanding provision (new standalone program; not inserted into FSRIA Title IX).
- **Numbers:** Authorization of appropriations: $50,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2030 (DISCRETIONARY — "authorized to be appropriated," not mandatory). Federal share 90%. Goal ≥300,000 trees/year. Maintenance/monitoring up to 3 years.
- **Dates:** Program established within 90 days of enactment; appropriations FY2027–FY2030.
- **Type:** NEW.
- **Affected:** State/local governments, Tribes, nonprofits, retail power providers (eligible grantees); high-energy-burden/low-canopy communities (intended beneficiaries).
- **Flag:** The only program in the title with an explicit new dollar authorization, and it is DISCRETIONARY (authorization of appropriations), capped at $50M/yr for FY2027–2030 (note: no FY2031, unlike the rest of the title's 2031 reauthorizations).

## Cross-cutting flags for this title
- **2031 reauthorization sweep:** Nearly every surviving FSRIA Title IX program is extended by striking 2023/2024/2026 and inserting 2031 (Secs. 9002 BioPreferred, 9003 Biorefinery, 9005 Bioenergy/Advanced Biofuels, 9008 Feedstock Flexibility, 9009 BCAP, 9007 REAP).
- **Mandatory vs. discretionary:** The four flagship bioeconomy programs (REAP, Bioenergy for Advanced Biofuels, Biorefinery, BioPreferred) are kept on their existing CCC/mandatory-funding footing by extending the funding-authorization year rather than converting them to "authorization of appropriations." IMPORTANT: the engrossed text supplied here does NOT restate specific mandatory annual dollar amounts for these programs — it shows only the date changes (and, for Sec. 9003, a per-project cap and an $18M rescission). The only NEW explicit dollar authorization in the title is the discretionary $50M/yr (FY2027–2030) tree-planting program (Sec. 9017).
- **Three repeals:** Biodiesel Fuel Education (Sec. 9006 / 7 U.S.C. 8106) and Carbon Utilization and Biogas Education (Sec. 9010 / 7 U.S.C. 8115) are repealed outright.
- **Hard dollar cuts:** $18,000,000 rescission of unobligated Biorefinery (9003) balances is the only affirmative rescission.
- **Dollar increases:** Biorefinery per-project loan-guarantee cap $250M → $400M (Sec. 9003); REAP grant cap (c)(3)(B) $25M → $50M (Sec. 9007).
- **Solar-on-farmland is the dominant new policy thrust:** A 2-year study (Sec. 9011) plus a binding funding prohibition (Sec. 9012) with acreage exceptions (<5 acres; <50 acres w/ on-farm-use + local approval), farmland conservation plans, full-repayment penalty, and a foreign-entity-of-concern (CHIPS Act) component-sourcing ban. This materially constrains how REAP and other USDA programs fund ground-mounted solar.
- **Agrivoltaics encouraged but farmland-conversion restricted:** REAP outreach (Sec. 9007(e)) and the solar study (Sec. 9011) promote shared crop/livestock + renewable energy on the same land, while Sec. 9012 bars funding that converts farmland — a deliberate push toward co-location over conversion.
- **SAF mainstreaming:** SAF added to the advanced-biofuel definition (Sec. 9001) and given a Department-wide strategy (Sec. 9013).
- **Section-numbering caution:** Bill section numbers (9001–9017) differ from the FSRIA section numbers they create/amend. Bill Sec. 9011 creates FSRIA sec. 9015; bill Sec. 9015 amends FSRIA sec. 9001(15)(A); bill Sec. 9012 creates FSRIA sec. 9016; bill Sec. 9016 creates FSRIA sec. 9017.
- **New studies/reports:** Solar-on-farmland (2 yr), second-use batteries (1 yr), BioPreferred NAICS/NAPCS report (90 days), plus pumping-system tools/education (180 days each).
