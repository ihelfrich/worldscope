# Title VIII — Forestry — Notes

## Title-level summary
Title VIII reauthorizes core USDA Forest Service / cooperative-forestry programs through FY2031 (most "2023→2031" date strikes) and substantially expands forest-management and wildfire authorities. Its most consequential and controversial provisions cluster in Subtitle D (Forest Management): multiple new and enlarged NEPA categorical exclusions (high-priority hazard trees, electric utility ROW, fuel-reduction activities), tripling of several existing CE acreage caps from 3,000 to 10,000 acres, an ESA Section 7 reconsultation carve-out for land/use management plans, and an electric-utility CE that exempts activities from ESA Section 7 and National Historic Preservation Act review entirely. Subtitle F (White Oak Resilience Act) and Subtitle G (Save Our Sequoias Act) create new freestanding programs; SOS declares a statutory 7-year "emergency" across named Sierra Nevada forests/parks with its own categorical exclusion (2,000-acre grove / 3,000-acre adjacent caps). Other subtitles touch agroforestry, forest inventory/carbon accounting, timber innovation/biochar, grazing-for-fuels, good-neighbor authority expansion, and a $220M timber-mill loan-guarantee program. The recurring environmental-law flags are NEPA CE expansions, ESA reconsultation/consultation waivers, and the statutory emergency designation.

### Subtitle D — Forest Management — Part I (National Forest System Management)

### Sec. 8401 — Categorical exclusion for high priority hazard trees
- **Does:** Directs Secretary (within 1 yr) to develop a NEPA categorical exclusion for "high-priority hazard tree activities" (pruning, felling, disposal). Must comply with NEPA and apply 36 CFR 220.6 extraordinary-circumstances procedures.
- **Amends:** New freestanding CE; references NEPA Sec. 111 (42 U.S.C. 4336e) and NEPA generally (42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq.).
- **Numbers:** Project size cap **6,000 acres**. High-priority hazard tree must be within 300 feet of NFS road (maintenance level 3/4/5), along an NFS trail, or in a developed recreation site.
- **Dates:** CE developed within 1 yr of enactment.
- **Type:** NEW (NEPA categorical exclusion)
- **Affected:** Forest Service; recreation/road users; environmental litigants.
- **Flag:** **NOTABLE — new NEPA CE, 6,000-acre cap.** Excludes wilderness/wilderness study areas, permanent road/trail construction, congressionally/proclamation-restricted lands, inventoried roadless areas, and uses inconsistent with forest plan.

### Sec. 8406 — Categorical exclusion for electric utility lines rights-of-way
- **Does:** Establishes new NEPA CE (from EA/EIS under NEPA Sec. 102) for development/approval and routine implementation of vegetation-management/facility-inspection/O&M plans under FLPMA Sec. 512(c)(1). No acreage cap stated. Prohibits permanent-road establishment (allows maintenance of existing; temporary roads decommissioned within 3 yrs).
- **Amends:** New freestanding CE; references NEPA Sec. 102 (42 U.S.C. 4332), FLPMA Sec. 512(c)(1) (43 U.S.C. 1772(c)(1)).
- **Dates:** Temporary-road decommission within 3 yrs of completion.
- **Type:** NEW (NEPA categorical exclusion)
- **Affected:** Electric utilities (winners); environmental/historic-preservation review process (losers).
- **Flag:** **HIGHLY NOTABLE — CE activities are NOT subject to ESA Section 7 (16 U.S.C. 1536) OR Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act.** Excludes Wilderness System components and congressionally-restricted vegetation-removal lands.

### Sec. 8407 — Forest management activities on National Forest System lands
- **Does:** Broad freestanding authority for Secretary to conduct forest management activities on NFS land for multiple ecosystem benefits. Establishes a NEPA categorical exclusion for fuel-reduction forest management activities, subject to size/coordination/forest-plan limits. Adds cooperative-authority for contracts/agreements on Federal and non-Federal land.
- **Amends:** New freestanding provision; references NEPA (42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq.); FRRRPA (16 U.S.C. 1406, 1609(a)).
- **Numbers:** CE limited to **≤10,000 acres, including not more than 3,000 acres of mechanical thinning.**
- **Type:** NEW (forest-management authority + NEPA CE)
- **Affected:** Forest Service; local governments (coordination partners).
- **Flag:** **HIGHLY NOTABLE — new fuel-reduction NEPA CE up to 10,000 acres (3,000 acres mechanical thinning).** No express extraordinary-circumstances or wilderness/roadless carve-out stated in CE text.

### Sec. 8408 — Suppression of wildfires
- **Does:** On specified high-risk NFS lands, directs Secretary (through FS Chief) to use available resources to contain detected wildfires within 24 hours of detection; bars inhibiting State/local suppression; restricts use of fire as resource tool to compliant prescribed fire; limits backfires/burnouts to incident-commander order or firefighter safety; requires updating prescribed-fire policy per Sept 2022 "National Prescribed Fire Program Review."
- **Amends:** New freestanding provision; references FRRRPA (16 U.S.C. 1609(a)).
- **Numbers/criteria:** Applies to lands at National Wildland Fire Preparedness Level 5; D2/D3/D4 drought intensity; or firesheds in top 10% of wildfire exposure.
- **Type:** NEW/REFORM (suppression mandate)
- **Affected:** Forest Service fire management (constrains managed/let-burn fire on covered lands).
- **Flag:** **NOTABLE — statutory 24-hour suppression mandate; limits use of "let-burn"/managed fire on high-risk lands.**

### Sec. 8409 — Kaibab National Forest restoration
- **Does:** Authorizes emergency acquisition/contracting flexibilities (48 CFR part 18) for White Sage Fire response in Kaibab NF (management/restoration, rebuilding, grounds/structure improvements, recovery), notwithstanding emergency/disaster-declaration prerequisites. Requires 180-day periodic reports on expenditures/contractors/conflicts/waste-fraud-abuse. Allows 12-month extension (subject to congressional approval) if a new wildfire impacts recovery.
- **Amends:** New freestanding provision.
- **Dates:** Authority expires earlier of 5 yrs after enactment or completion of recovery.
- **Type:** NEW (site-specific emergency contracting)
- **Affected:** Kaibab NF / White Sage Fire area.
- **Flag:** — (transparency reporting built in)

### Subtitle D — Part II (Forest Management Activities)

### Sec. 8411 — No additional consultation required
- **Does:** Provides that Secretary shall NOT be required to reinitiate ESA Section 7(a)(2) consultation (or 50 CFR 402.16) on a land/resource management plan (FS) or land use plan (BLM) when (a) a new species is listed/critical habitat designated, or (b) new information reveals plan effects on listed species/critical habitat not previously considered.
- **Amends:** Sec. 6(d)(2), FRRRPA (16 U.S.C. 1604(d)(2)) [Forest Service]; Sec. 202, FLPMA (43 U.S.C. 1712) — adds new subsection (g) [BLM].
- **Type:** REFORM (ESA consultation carve-out)
- **Affected:** Forest Service, BLM (relieved of reconsultation); listed species (potential losers).
- **Flag:** **HIGHLY NOTABLE / CONTROVERSIAL — ESA Section 7 reconsultation waiver for land-use/management plans upon new listings or new information ("notwithstanding any other provision of law").**

### Sec. 8412 — Good neighbor authority
- **Does:** Expands Good Neighbor Authority to add Indian Tribe, special district, and county as eligible cooperators (special district newly defined). Adds authority to use retained receipts to construct **new permanent roads** on Federal lands (if necessary to authorized restoration and approved via environmental analysis or CE), and to replace/decommission existing roads harming forest/rangeland/watershed health. Extends a sunset (2028 → 2030). Strikes paragraph (4). Applies retroactively to projects initiated after the 2018 Farm Bill enactment.
- **Amends:** Sec. 8206, Agricultural Act of 2014 (16 U.S.C. 2113a).
- **Dates:** receipts-use authority 2028 → 2030.
- **Type:** REFORM/INCREASE (expanded GNA authority)
- **Affected:** Tribes, special districts, counties (winners); enables new permanent-road construction.
- **Flag:** **NOTABLE — new authority to build new permanent roads on Federal land using GNA receipts** (CE/environmental-analysis approval required).

## Cross-cutting flags for this title

- **NEPA categorical-exclusion expansions (acreage caps):**
  - Sec. 8401 — new high-priority hazard tree CE: **6,000 acres**.
  - Sec. 8402 — collaborative restoration CE: **3,000 → 10,000 acres**.
  - Sec. 8403 — wildfire resilience CE: **3,000 → 10,000 acres**.
  - Sec. 8404 — fuel-break CE: **3,000 → 10,000 acres**.
  - Sec. 8405 — sage-grouse/mule deer CE: **4,500 acres (forested) / 7,500 acres (rangeland)**; single-species sufficient.
  - Sec. 8406 — new electric-utility ROW CE: no acreage cap stated.
  - Sec. 8407 — new fuel-reduction CE: **≤10,000 acres (≤3,000 acres mechanical thinning)**.
  - Sec. 8705 (SOS) — new Protection Project CE: **2,000 acres in groves / 3,000 acres adjacent**.
- **ESA Section 7 consultation changes:**
  - Sec. 8411 — reconsultation NOT required on FS land-management or BLM land-use plans on new listings/critical-habitat designations or new information ("notwithstanding any other provision of law").
  - Sec. 8406 — utility-ROW CE activities NOT subject to ESA Section 7.
  - Sec. 8705 — incorporates frozen-date ESA consultation regs (50 CFR 402.05 as of July 21, 2022).
- **National Historic Preservation Act waivers/exemptions:**
  - Sec. 8406 — utility-ROW CE NOT subject to NHPA Sec. 106.
  - Sec. 8509 — communications-use authorizations in previously analyzed areas fully exempt from NEPA and NHPA; no reconsideration on new information.
- **NEPA exemptions (beyond CEs):**
  - Sec. 8704 — Giant Sequoia Assessment exempt from NEPA.
  - Sec. 8509 — communications-use exemption from NEPA.
- **Judicial-review / litigation linkage:** Sec. 8705 incorporates IIJA Sec. 40807(d),(e) emergency-action rules (which contain litigation/judicial-review provisions) into giant sequoia Protection Projects — captured by cross-reference; the text itself does not restate explicit litigation limits.
- **Statutory "emergency" designation:** Sec. 8705 — Congress legislatively declares a 7-year emergency across named Sierra Nevada national forests, monuments, and parks to expedite logging/fuels work.
- **Expanded logging / forest-management authorities:** Sec. 8416 (no-appraisal timber disposal during extreme-risk events), Sec. 8417 (utility cutting without separate timber sale), Sec. 8421 ($220M mill loan guarantees tied to Federal-land vegetation removal), Sec. 8407 (broad forest-management authority), Sec. 8412 (new permanent-road construction via Good Neighbor Authority receipts), Sec. 8710 (stewardship contracting extended into national parks).
- **Reauthorization year:** Most extended programs run through **FY2031** (typical date strike 2023→2031); several new programs/authorities sunset **7 years after enactment** (Secs. 8434, 8603, 8604, 8705 emergency, 8711) or **Sept 30, 2031** (Sec. 8414).
- **Dollar figures:** $10M/yr State assessment cap (8101); $7M/yr agroforestry (8301); $5M/yr nurseries (8305); $55,000 timber-sale threshold (8416); $220,000,000 mill loan guarantees (8421); $5M Community Wood grant cap (8431); 75% advance / 35% biochar facility / 50% matching figures across various sections; ≥15% Tribal set-aside (8711).
- **Winners:** Forest Service/timber industry, electric utilities, grazing permittees, sawmills/wood-processing, Tribes (esp. Tule River), rural fire departments, nurseries. **Potential losers:** ESA-listed species, environmental/historic-preservation review processes, parties relying on NEPA/judicial review.
