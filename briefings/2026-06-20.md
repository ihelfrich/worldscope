## Macro Situation

![Yield Curve](briefings/2026-06-20-yield_curve.png)

The Warsh Fed's June 17 decision to hold at 3.50-3.75% arrived freighted with structural significance beyond the rate itself. [Kevin Warsh](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/06/18/treasury-yields-investors-warsh-fed-interest-rates.html) eliminated forward guidance - the primary communications tool of the Powell and Bernanke eras - and announced five task forces to rethink the monetary policy framework. This is not an incremental change: it removes a major anchor for term premiums and means markets must price the policy path through inflation and growth data alone, without official signaling. The 2-year Treasury at 4.179% is now above the 10-year at 4.453%, but the spread has narrowed to +0.41 from deeper inversion earlier in the year.

The Iran war's oil shock is the central macro disturbance. [Crude surged 36-39%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/31/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) from late February through March as fighting in the Gulf threatened Hormuz. The June 19 framework deal introduces a conditional Hormuz reopening, but the nuclear negotiation runs 60 days with no guarantee of completion. The current consensus among FOMC participants is that a rate hike - not a cut - is the next most likely move if Iran-sourced inflation proves sticky. The Fed [now projects only one 25bp cut for the remainder of 2026](https://www.unboxfuture.com/2026/06/fed-june-2026-hawkish-pause-rates-held.html), versus multiple cuts in the earlier baseline.

Underlying US fundamentals heading into the June data cycle: May unemployment at [4.3%](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/UNRATE), May nonfarm payrolls at 159,001K, core PCE last reported 129.63 (April), [M2 at $22,804B](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/M2SL) (April). The labor market is cooling slowly. The Fed balance sheet stands at $6.71T as of early June, declining under QT. JOLTS job openings for April were 7,618K - down from cycle highs but not recessionary. Q1 2026 real GDP grew at [2.0% annualized](https://www.usbank.com/investing/financial-perspectives/market-news/federal-reserve-interest-rate.html), supported by private investment and exports, a figure that may be revised as the tariff disruption works through.

The most important cross-asset development of the past week is not the Fed decision itself but the yen move. USD/JPY collapsed from roughly 160 in early June to 144.58 as of this morning - a 10% yen strengthening in six weeks. The BOJ [held at 0.5% on June 17](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/boj-hold-interest-rates-at-05-us-retail-sales-sink-yen-steady-202506171352) but Deputy Governor Himino explicitly flagged more hikes ahead. Real Japanese rates remain deeply negative. The yen move has not been driven by BOJ action; it is more likely a safe-haven rotation and Hormuz-risk-premium unwind as the Iran framework advanced. If Hormuz fully reopens, energy-import-sensitive currencies including yen and INR should continue to appreciate against the dollar.

## United States Politics and Policy

The Supreme Court's term is entering its final weeks with 22 cases pending, including the most politically explosive docket in a decade. Among the outstanding cases are [President Trump's birthright citizenship executive order](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/09/nx-s1-5847967/supreme-court-major-cases-left-2026), campaign finance limits, mail-in ballot rules, and Second Amendment cases. The Court's pace has accelerated: on June 18 it issued a unanimous ruling that prosecuting a marijuana user for firearms possession is inconsistent with the Second Amendment, a narrow case but one with implications for the felon-in-possession statute's scope. Major term-end opinions are expected by late June or early July.

The tariff landscape is the most active legislative and legal terrain. The [IEEPA tariff ruling](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/02/supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs-key-takeaways-and-implications-for-importers/) (February 20, 6-3) invalidated $175-179B in collected tariff revenue and opens refund litigation. Trump's pivot to Section 122 (Trade Act of 1974) and Section 301 is legally more defensible but politically constrained by the statute's 150-day cap on Section 122 measures. The [June 3 USTR forced-labor Section 301 investigation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/03/us-tariffs-60-economies-dection-301-forced-labor-trade-practices-.html) covering 60 economies is a way of reloading tariff authority while the administration navigates the IEEPA aftermath. The US-China 30% tariff deal announced June 11 is a 60-day pause structure similar to the Geneva US-Iran framework: it delays escalation rather than resolving the underlying dispute.

The June 2026 **Secure America Act** provided $69.5B for ICE and CBP through 2029, a follow-on to the "One Big Beautiful Bill" (signed July 4, 2025) which contained $325B for immigration enforcement and defense plus the Trump tax cut extensions. The reconciliation path is exhausted for now; any further major spending requires 60 Senate votes or another procedural mechanism. Congress is in recess until late June.

The **Court of International Trade** continues to hear tariff challenges. The [Trade Compliance Resource Hub](https://www.tradecomplianceresourcehub.com/2026/06/03/trump-2-0-tariff-tracker/) documents active CIT dockets covering Section 232 steel/aluminum derivatives, USMCA preference claims, and antidumping reviews. The CIT's Section 122 jurisdictional question - whether the 150-day clock runs from original imposition or from the IEEPA termination date - may be the next landmark ruling.

Turkey's inflation at 32.61% in May 2026 is accelerating rather than disinflating, primarily because of energy import costs and pre-election fiscal loosening. Erdogan's stated single-digit inflation target for 2026 is definitively off-track.

### Russia and Post-Soviet Space

Russia's economic position in mid-2026 is one of managed deterioration. Oil revenues are 27% below October 2024 levels despite the Iran-war price spike providing a partial offset. The UK's June 14 interception of the Smyrtos established a new legal and operational precedent for shadow-fleet enforcement: Royal Marine commandos physically boarding a vessel on the high seas. The diplomatic implications are significant - Russia lodged a formal protest, calling it "piracy" - but London argues the vessel was operating in violation of UK/EU sanctions and that the English Channel gives the UK sufficient jurisdictional basis.

The EU's 21st sanctions package, proposed June 9 and likely to be adopted by end of June, blacklists 30 additional shadow-fleet vessels and restricts LNG tanker access to EU ports. The cumulative effect on Novatek's Arctic LNG 2 project is mounting: insurance, tanker access, and port-call restrictions are creating structural obstacles that are qualitatively different from the initial 2022-2023 sanction waves [high]. Russia's CBR ruble rate at 73.44/USD today is partially a function of mandatory export conversion requirements rather than genuine market strength.

Georgia continues to face internal political pressure over its democratic backsliding, with EU accession talks effectively frozen. The South Caucasus remains an area of latent risk given the unresolved Armenian-Azerbaijani status questions.

Iran's domestic calculus is the critical unknown.
