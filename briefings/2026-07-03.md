## United States politics and policy

The Federal Register of June 25 carried two executive orders that deserve immediate cross-sector attention. The first, "[Ushering in the Next Frontier of Quantum Innovation](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/25/2026-12910/ushering-in-the-next-frontier-of-quantum-innovation)," establishes a national strategic framework for quantum computing leadership, with implications for semiconductor supply chains, export controls, and the bilateral technology race with China. The second, "[Securing the Nation Against Advanced Cryptographic Attacks](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/25/2026-12909/securing-the-nation-against-advanced-cryptographic-attacks)," mandates post-quantum cryptography migration across federal systems — a direct response to the growing credible timeline for quantum computers capable of breaking RSA-2048. These two EOs together represent the first systematic US government policy posture acknowledging that the cryptographic infrastructure of the internet has a finite lifespan. The structural implication for financial institutions, defense contractors, and critical infrastructure operators is significant [high].

The Federal Reserve's banking supervisory framework received a material change: the [prohibition on the use of "reputation risk" in bank supervision](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/25/2026-12856/prohibition-on-the-use-of-reputation-risk) was finalized, implementing [Executive Order 14331 ("Guaranteeing Fair Banking for All Americans")](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/25/2026-12856/prohibition-on-the-use-of-reputation-risk) by codifying its removal from supervisory frameworks at NCUA, OCC, and Federal Reserve. This is the regulatory conclusion of the "Operation Choke Point" litigation and political battle — financial regulators can no longer use a bank's public reputation associations as a supervisory factor, which has practical implications for crypto firms, firearms dealers, and cannabis-adjacent businesses previously disadvantaged by informal pressure.

On campaign finance, the FEC data through June 25 shows Senator **Jon Ossoff** (D-GA) has raised [$81.15M for the 2026 cycle](https://www.fec.gov/data/candidate/S8GA00180/), the largest Senate warchest on the board. **James Talarico** (D-TX Senate) has reached $40.28M, **Cory Booker** (D-NJ) $32.26M, and **Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez** (D-NY) $31.1M for her House seat. Speaker **Mike Johnson** has raised $17.55M — below the Democratic challengers in open-seat Senate races but within the norms for a sitting House Speaker in a non-presidential midterm.

The administration's war supplemental is the most consequential pending fiscal action: the White House formally requested [$87.6 billion from Congress](https://www.pravda.com.ua/news/2026/06/25/8041019/) to replenish Pentagon stocks depleted in the Iran war.
