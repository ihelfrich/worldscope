## Cyber and Biosecurity

**FIRST-OF-KIND KEV CALLOUT**: CVE-2026-42271 in **BerriAI LiteLLM** is the first LLM infrastructure proxy framework to appear on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. [LiteLLM](https://securityaffairs.com/193343/security/u-s-cisa-adds-berriai-litellm-and-check-point-security-gateway-flaws-to-its-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog.html) acts as a middleware layer routing requests between applications and AI model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Azure OpenAI, Bedrock, etc.), meaning a compromised LiteLLM instance is positioned between the application and the model's API key vault. The structural signal is not merely "an AI tool was hacked" but that the AI proxy-and-orchestration layer has now been confirmed as an active threat surface in real-world attacks. When LangFlow appeared on KEV in May 2026 (CVE-2025-34291), that was the first agentic workflow framework; LiteLLM is the first AI gateway/proxy framework. The two together define a new category of infrastructure risk: AI plumbing, not just AI endpoints.

The exploit chain makes this doubly serious: CVE-2026-42271 (CVSS 8.8, credentialed) chains with the Starlette BadHost flaw (CVE-2026-48710) to produce a [CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated RCE](https://cybelangel.com/blog/itellm-vulnerability-cve-2026-42271/), confirmed by Horizon3.ai. The Qilin ransomware group is the attributed threat actor. Federal agency deadline is June 22; the patch has been available since May 8 (LiteLLM v1.83.7). Organizations running LiteLLM in production should treat this as P0.

**Check Point Security Gateway** (CVE-2026-50751) also made the June 8 KEV update. Check Point VPN is standard enterprise network perimeter; the June 11 federal deadline has now passed. Exploitation of Check Point VPNs has historically preceded lateral movement into government and defense contractor networks.
